### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular arginine levels and increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. To explore this, we need to design an experiment that modulates intracellular arginine levels and assesses the yeast's resistance to caffeine. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into how arginine metabolism influences cellular stress responses.

### Hypothesis
Lower intracellular arginine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae lead to increased resistance to caffeine, potentially due to changes in metabolic pathways or stress response mechanisms that are influenced by arginine availability.

### Experimental Plan

#### Setup
- **Base Media**: Use minimal media without amino acids to control for baseline arginine levels.
- **Temperature**: Maintain all cultures at 30°C.
- **Duration**: Conduct the experiment for 20 hours.

#### Supplementation Levels
1. **Control (No Arginine Supplementation)**
   - Baseline condition to observe natural intracellular arginine levels.
2. **Low Arginine Supplementation**
   - Add 0.1 mM L-arginine to the media. This concentration is chosen to moderately increase intracellular arginine levels without reaching saturation.
3. **High Arginine Supplementation**
   - Add 1.0 mM L-arginine to the media, a concentration expected to saturate arginine uptake systems.

#### Caffeine Treatment Levels
1. **No Caffeine (Control)**
   - Baseline condition for growth without caffeine stress.
2. **Caffeine Treatment**
   - Add 5 mM caffeine, a concentration known to impose stress on yeast cells, to assess resistance.

#### Negative Control
- **Serine Supplementation**
  - Add 1.0 mM L-serine in parallel experiments as a negative control. Serine is not directly involved in pathways linked to caffeine resistance and will help validate that any observed effects are specific to arginine manipulation.

#### Experimental Combinations
- **Control (No Arginine, No Caffeine)**
- **Control (No Arginine, + Caffeine)**
- **Low Arginine, No Caffeine**
- **Low Arginine, + Caffeine**
- **High Arginine, No Caffeine**
- **High Arginine, + Caffeine**
- **Serine Control, No Caffeine**
- **Serine Control, + Caffeine**

#### Measurements
- **Growth Curves**: Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth dynamics and viability.
- **Metabolomics**: At experiment termination, use mass spectrometry to quantify intracellular arginine levels and confirm modulation.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Control (No Arginine, + Caffeine)**: Expect baseline resistance to caffeine.
- **Low Arginine, + Caffeine**: If the hypothesis is correct, expect increased resistance to caffeine compared to the control with no arginine.
- **High Arginine, + Caffeine**: Expect a return to baseline or decreased resistance if high arginine levels negate the resistance mechanism.
- **Serine Controls**: Should show no significant change in caffeine resistance, confirming the specificity of arginine's role.

This experiment will elucidate the relationship between intracellular arginine levels and caffeine resistance, providing insights into the metabolic or stress response pathways involved.
