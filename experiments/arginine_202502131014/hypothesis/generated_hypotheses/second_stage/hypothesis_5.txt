**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular arginine levels and increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic pathways and stress response mechanisms of yeast. 

**Hypothesis:**
Lower intracellular arginine levels are associated with increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. This could be due to arginine's role in cellular metabolism and stress response pathways, potentially affecting the cell's ability to detoxify or withstand caffeine-induced stress.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Base media: Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Arginine supplementation:
     - Control: No arginine supplementation.
     - Low Arginine: 50 µM arginine.
     - High Arginine: 200 µM arginine.

3. **Caffeine Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No caffeine.
   - Low Caffeine: 2 mM caffeine.
   - High Caffeine: 5 mM caffeine.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use serine as a negative control at 200 µM, equimolar to the highest arginine supplementation level.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Each combination of arginine supplementation and caffeine treatment will be tested in separate wells.
   - Total conditions: 3 (arginine levels) x 3 (caffeine levels) + 2 (serine control with and without caffeine) = 11 conditions.

6. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Inoculate each well with the yeast strain in defined media conditions.
   - Add arginine or serine supplementation at t=0h.
   - Add caffeine treatment at t=0h.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular arginine levels at the end of the experiment.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If lower intracellular arginine levels are associated with increased resistance to caffeine, we expect to see better growth or higher viability in wells with low arginine supplementation and caffeine treatment compared to high arginine supplementation.
- The negative control (serine) should not show any specific increase in caffeine resistance, confirming the specificity of the arginine effect.
- Independent effects of arginine and caffeine should be observable, with low arginine potentially leading to altered growth profiles even in the absence of caffeine.
- The mass spectrometry results should confirm the modulation of intracellular arginine levels based on the supplementation provided.
