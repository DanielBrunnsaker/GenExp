### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular arginine levels and increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into arginine's role in stress response pathways and cellular resistance mechanisms.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular levels of arginine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae enhance the activation of stress response pathways, leading to increased resistance to caffeine. This may be due to a shift in metabolic flux or resource allocation favoring stress resistance mechanisms when arginine is limited.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Objective**: To test if lower intracellular arginine levels are associated with increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

2. **Experimental Setup**:
   - **Strain**: Use a wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.
   - **Media**: Minimal media without amino acids.
   - **Temperature**: 30°C, with shaking.

3. **Supplementation Levels**:
   - **Arginine Supplementation**:
     - Level 1: 0 mM (Control, no arginine supplementation)
     - Level 2: 0.5 mM (Low arginine)
     - Level 3: 1 mM (High arginine)

4. **Caffeine Treatment**:
   - **Caffeine Levels**:
     - Level 1: 0 mM (Control, no caffeine)
     - Level 2: 5 mM (Caffeine treatment)

5. **Negative Control**:
   - Supplementation with 1 mM alanine to ensure that any observed effects are specific to arginine and not due to general amino acid availability.

6. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Test all combinations of arginine supplementation and caffeine treatment, resulting in 6 experimental conditions (3 arginine levels x 2 caffeine levels).
   - Include negative control conditions with alanine supplementation at 1 mM, both with and without caffeine treatment.

7. **Measurement**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular arginine levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Resistance**: If lower intracellular arginine levels are associated with increased resistance to caffeine, we expect to see higher growth rates and viability in the 0 mM arginine condition with caffeine, compared to higher arginine levels.
- **Dose-Response**: A dose-response relationship may be observed, with varying levels of resistance correlating with the amount of arginine supplementation.
- **Metabolite Analysis**: Mass spectrometry should confirm lower intracellular arginine levels in the 0 mM supplementation condition, supporting the hypothesis that these levels contribute to increased caffeine resistance.
- **Negative Control**: The alanine supplementation should not show increased resistance to caffeine, confirming that the effect is specific to arginine levels.

This experimental design will help elucidate the relationship between intracellular arginine levels and stress resistance, specifically in the context of caffeine exposure.
