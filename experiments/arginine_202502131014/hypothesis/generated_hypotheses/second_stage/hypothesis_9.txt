### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular arginine levels and increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Arginine is a key amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways, and its intracellular concentration could influence cellular stress responses, including resistance to chemicals like caffeine. Understanding this association could provide insights into metabolic regulation and stress response mechanisms in yeast.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular levels of arginine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with increased resistance to caffeine, possibly due to altered metabolic pathways or stress response mechanisms that are modulated by arginine availability.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Arginine Supplementation:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No arginine supplementation.
   - Level 2: Supplement with 0.5 mM arginine.
   - Level 3: Supplement with 1.0 mM arginine.

3. **Caffeine Treatment:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No caffeine.
   - Level 2: Add 5 mM caffeine.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use serine at 1.0 mM as a negative control for amino acid supplementation, as serine is not directly involved in pathways that could influence caffeine resistance.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Each combination of arginine supplementation and caffeine treatment should be tested in its own well.
   - Include both supplemented and unsupplemented conditions for each level of caffeine treatment.
   - Include serine negative controls under both caffeine and non-caffeine conditions.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and determine growth curves.
   - At the end of the experiment (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to quantify intracellular arginine levels.

7. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Include an additional set of wells with only serine supplementation to verify that serine does not independently affect caffeine resistance.

### Expected Outcomes

- If lower intracellular arginine levels are associated with increased caffeine resistance, we would expect to see a higher growth rate or final cell density in the wells with lower arginine supplementation (Level 1) under caffeine treatment (Level 2), compared to higher arginine levels.
- The negative control (serine) should not significantly affect caffeine resistance, confirming that the observed effects are specific to arginine modulation.
- The metabolomics analysis should verify that intracellular arginine levels correlate with the supplementation levels, confirming that the experimental setup successfully modulates arginine levels.

By evaluating these outcomes, we can support or refute the association between intracellular arginine levels and caffeine resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
