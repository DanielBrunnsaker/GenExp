### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular arginine levels and increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Arginine is a key amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways, and its intracellular concentration could potentially influence cellular responses to external stressors, such as caffeine. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic adaptability of yeast cells under chemical stress.

### Hypothesis

Cells with reduced intracellular arginine levels exhibit increased resistance to caffeine due to altered metabolic pathways that enhance stress response mechanisms. Specifically, lower arginine levels may lead to the activation of pathways that enhance cellular detoxification or repair processes.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Prepare minimal media without amino acids for baseline conditions.

2. **Supplementation Levels**:
   - **Arginine Supplementation**:
     - Control: No arginine supplementation.
     - Low Arginine: 0.1 mM arginine.
     - High Arginine: 1 mM arginine.
   - **Negative Control**: Use alanine at 1 mM as a negative control, as it is not directly involved in the pathways influenced by caffeine or arginine.

3. **Caffeine Treatment**:
   - Control: No caffeine.
   - Treatment: 5 mM caffeine, a concentration known to induce stress without completely inhibiting growth.

4. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Total Conditions: 3 arginine levels x 2 caffeine levels = 6 conditions, plus 2 negative controls (alanine with and without caffeine).
   - Culture each condition in a 96-well plate format, with predefined replicates, shaking settings, and inoculation concentrations.
   - Start all treatments and supplementations at t=0h.

5. **Measurements**:
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth curves and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular arginine concentrations.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Growth and Viability**: Cells with lower arginine supplementation levels (0.1 mM) are expected to exhibit increased resistance to caffeine, reflected in higher growth rates or viability compared to the high arginine condition.
- **Metabolomics**: Mass spectrometry should confirm lower intracellular arginine levels in the low arginine supplementation condition.
- **Negative Control**: Alanine supplementation is not expected to influence caffeine resistance, serving as a baseline to ensure observed effects are specific to arginine modulation.

This experimental setup will allow us to evaluate the association between intracellular arginine levels and caffeine resistance, while controlling for confounding factors through appropriate negative controls.
