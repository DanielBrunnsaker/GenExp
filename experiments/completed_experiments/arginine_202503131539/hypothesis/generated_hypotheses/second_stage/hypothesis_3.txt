### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular levels of arginine and increased resistance to caffeine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Arginine is a basic amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways, including the urea cycle and polyamine synthesis. Understanding how arginine levels influence resistance to caffeine, a known stressor, can provide insights into cellular stress response mechanisms.

### Hypothesis
Cells with reduced intracellular arginine levels exhibit increased resistance to caffeine due to altered metabolic states that enhance stress tolerance mechanisms. This may involve changes in the expression of stress response genes or modulation of metabolic pathways that confer resistance to caffeine-induced stress.

### Experimental Plan

**1. Experimental Setup:**
- Use a wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
- Culture in minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
- Conduct experiments at 30°C with shaking to ensure aeration.

**2. Arginine Supplementation Levels:**
- Control: No arginine supplementation.
- Low Arginine: 0.1 mM arginine.
- High Arginine: 2 mM arginine (supraphysiological concentration).

**3. Caffeine Treatment Levels:**
- Control: No caffeine treatment.
- Caffeine Treatment: 5 mM caffeine.

**4. Negative Control:**
- Use serine as a negative control, provided at 2 mM (equimolar to the highest arginine supplementation).

**5. Experimental Conditions:**
- Combine each arginine supplementation level with each caffeine treatment level, resulting in six conditions.
- Include treated and untreated negative controls (serine) for a total of eight conditions.

**6. Measurements:**
- Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth.
- At the experiment's conclusion (20 hours), measure intracellular arginine concentrations and other relevant metabolites using mass spectrometry-based metabolomics.

**7. Verification:**
- Use mass spectrometry to verify that arginine supplementation modulates intracellular arginine levels as expected.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Increased Resistance with Low Arginine:** If the hypothesis is correct, cells with low arginine supplementation (0.1 mM) should show increased resistance to caffeine, evidenced by higher growth rates or viability compared to the control and high arginine conditions when exposed to caffeine.
- **Impact of High Arginine:** High arginine supplementation (2 mM) may not confer the same resistance, potentially due to saturation of pathways that do not contribute to stress resistance.
- **Negative Control:** The serine control should not display increased resistance to caffeine, confirming that the observed effects are specific to arginine modulation.
- **Independent Effects:** Arginine supplementation could independently affect growth or stress response pathways, which should be evident in the absence of caffeine treatment. 

This experimental design will help determine if there is a causal link between intracellular arginine levels and caffeine resistance, while controlling for potential confounding factors.
