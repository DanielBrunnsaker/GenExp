**Hypothesis:**
Cells with lower intracellular arginine levels exhibit increased resistance to caffeine due to altered metabolic pathways that enhance stress response mechanisms.    - High Arginine (C3): Supplement with 5 mM arginine (supraphysiological level).

3. **Caffeine Treatment:**
   - Control (T1): No caffeine.
   - Caffeine Treatment (T2): Add 5 mM caffeine.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use alanine at 5 mM as the negative control, as it is not directly involved in stress response pathways, providing a baseline for any non-specific effects.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total of 6 conditions (3 arginine levels x 2 caffeine treatments).
   - Each condition should be set up in a separate well of a 96-well plate.
   - Include negative control wells with alanine supplementation under both caffeine and no caffeine conditions.

6. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Inoculate each well with a standardized concentration of the reference/wild-type prototrophic strain.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular arginine levels at the end of the experiment.

- If the hypothesis is correct, cells with lower intracellular arginine (C2) should show increased resistance to caffeine (i.e., maintain growth better in the presence of caffeine compared to C1 and C3).

- The negative control with alanine should not show any significant changes in growth resistance to caffeine, confirming that the observed effects are specific to arginine modulation.
- The results will help determine if there is a dose-dependent relationship between arginine levels and caffeine resistance, supporting or refuting the association stated.
