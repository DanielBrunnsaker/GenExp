### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to the chemical spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into how glutamate metabolism influences cellular stress responses. This experiment aims to explore the potential connection by modulating intracellular glutamate levels and observing the yeast cells' response to spermine exposure.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular glutamate levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will lead to decreased resistance to spermine. The proposed mechanism is that elevated glutamate levels may affect cellular pathways involved in stress response or detoxification, potentially altering the cell's ability to cope with chemical stressors like spermine.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.

2. **Glutamate Supplementation**:
   - **Control**: No glutamate supplementation.
   - **Low Glutamate**: Supplement media with 1 mM L-glutamate.
   - **High Glutamate**: Supplement media with 5 mM L-glutamate.
   - Justification: Glutamate supplementation will increase intracellular glutamate levels, as yeast can uptake extracellular glutamate.

3. **Spermine Treatment**:
   - Add 1 mM spermine to each well at the start of the experiment (t=0h).

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use alanine as a negative control, supplementing at 5 mM to match the highest glutamate concentration. Alanine is not expected to influence spermine resistance significantly.

5. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and determine viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels.

6. **Temperature and Conditions**:
   - Incubate all samples at 30°C with shaking to ensure consistent growth conditions.

7. **Replicates**:
   - Ensure sufficient replicates for statistical significance.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Glutamate Levels**: If higher intracellular glutamate levels lead to decreased resistance to spermine, we expect to see reduced growth or viability in the high glutamate supplementation group compared to the control and low glutamate groups.
- **Control and Alanine Supplementation**: These groups should show typical resistance levels to spermine, serving as baselines for comparison.
- **Independent Effects**: The experiment will also reveal any independent effects of glutamate supplementation on growth, regardless of spermine exposure, by comparing the growth curves and metabolomics data across different conditions.
