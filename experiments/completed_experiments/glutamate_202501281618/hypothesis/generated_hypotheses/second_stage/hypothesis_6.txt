### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to the chemical spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic pathways influencing yeast's response to chemical stressors. By modulating intracellular glutamate levels, we can test this association and evaluate its impact on the yeast's resistance to spermine.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular glutamate levels will exhibit decreased resistance to spermine compared to cells with normal glutamate levels. This could be due to glutamate's role in cellular stress responses, where higher levels may alter metabolic pathways that affect the cell's ability to detoxify or tolerate spermine.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without any amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.

2. **Supplementation:**
   - Modulate intracellular glutamate levels using L-glutamate supplementation.
   - Three conditions will be tested:
     - Control: No glutamate supplementation.
     - Low Glutamate: 0.5 mM L-glutamate.
     - High Glutamate: 2 mM L-glutamate.
   - Include a negative control using 2 mM L-alanine, which is not expected to affect spermine resistance.

3. **Chemical Treatment:**
   - At t=0h, add spermine to all wells at a concentration of 1 mM, a level known to challenge yeast cells without causing immediate lethality.

4. **Measurements:**
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels and verify the effectiveness of supplementation.

5. **Controls and Replicates:**
   - Include appropriate controls for each condition and ensure multiple biological replicates for statistical robustness.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Group (No Glutamate Supplementation):** Expected to have baseline resistance to spermine, serving as a reference point.
- **Low Glutamate Supplementation:** May show a slight decrease in resistance to spermine, reflecting a modest increase in intracellular glutamate levels.
- **High Glutamate Supplementation:** Expected to show a more pronounced decrease in resistance to spermine, supporting the hypothesis that higher glutamate levels are associated with decreased chemical resistance.
- **Negative Control (L-Alanine):** Should exhibit resistance similar to the control group, confirming that the observed effects are specific to glutamate supplementation.

These outcomes will help determine whether elevated intracellular glutamate levels correlate with decreased spermine resistance, providing evidence to either support or refute the initial statement.
