**Motivation:**

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to the chemical spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association can provide insights into the cellular mechanisms that regulate resistance to polyamines such as spermine, which are known to affect cell growth and viability.

**Hypothesis:**

Cells with increased intracellular glutamate levels exhibit decreased resistance to spermine due to glutamate's role in cellular stress responses. Specifically, higher glutamate levels may interfere with the cellular mechanisms that mitigate the toxic effects of spermine, leading to reduced cell viability when exposed to this compound.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.

2. **Supplementation:**
   - To modulate intracellular glutamate levels, supplement the media with 0 mM (control), 2 mM, and 5 mM glutamate. These concentrations are chosen to cover a range from no supplementation to physiologically relevant levels that can increase intracellular glutamate.

3. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with 5 mM alanine, which is not expected to influence spermine resistance or intracellular glutamate levels directly.

4. **Spermine Treatment:**
   - At t=0h, add spermine to a final concentration of 1 mM to each well, including the negative control. This concentration is chosen based on known inhibitory effects on yeast growth.

5. **Measurement:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels.

6. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Each condition (glutamate levels and controls) should be tested in triplicate to ensure statistical robustness.
   - Maintain all cultures at 30°C with shaking to ensure optimal growth conditions.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Increased Intracellular Glutamate:** If higher glutamate levels are associated with decreased resistance to spermine, we expect to see reduced growth rates and lower OD600 values in the 2 mM and 5 mM glutamate-supplemented wells compared to the control without glutamate.
- **Negative Control (Alanine):** The alanine control should exhibit growth rates similar to the 0 mM glutamate wells, indicating that the observed effects are specific to glutamate supplementation.
- **Metabolomics Data:** Mass spectrometry should confirm increased intracellular glutamate levels in the supplemented conditions, supporting the hypothesis that these levels correlate with decreased resistance to spermine.

This experimental design will help determine if there is a causal relationship between intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to spermine, providing insights into the underlying cellular mechanisms.
