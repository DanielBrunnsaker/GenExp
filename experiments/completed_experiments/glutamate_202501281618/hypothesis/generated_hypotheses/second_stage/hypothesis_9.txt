### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between increased intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to the polyamine compound spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic and stress response pathways in yeast, potentially revealing targets for modulating resistance to chemical stressors.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular glutamate levels under standard conditions exhibit decreased resistance to spermine due to altered polyamine metabolism or stress response pathways. This could be mediated by glutamate's role as a precursor in amino acid and nitrogen metabolism, which may modulate cellular response pathways involved in detoxification or stress resistance.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain. Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to establish baseline conditions.

2. **Supplementation**:
   - **Glutamate Supplementation**: Prepare two treatment levels of glutamate supplementation in the media:
     - Control: No glutamate added.
     - Low: 5 mM glutamate.
     - High: 10 mM glutamate.
   - Ensure that the glutamate is added at t=0h.

3. **Negative Control**:
   - Use serine as the negative control, added at 10 mM, as it is not directly involved in the pathways hypothesized to affect spermine resistance.

4. **Spermine Treatment**:
   - At t=0h, add spermine to each well at a concentration of 1 mM, which is known to induce stress in yeast cells.

5. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess resistance to spermine.
   - At the end of the 20-hour experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels and other relevant metabolites.

6. **Experimental Controls**:
   - Include wells with no spermine to account for growth differences due to glutamate supplementation alone.
   - Ensure that each condition is performed in triplicate to account for variability.

### Expected Outcomes

- **If the Hypothesis is Supported**: Cells supplemented with higher levels of glutamate may show reduced growth (lower OD600) in the presence of spermine compared to the control, suggesting decreased resistance. Metabolomics may reveal altered levels of metabolites involved in stress response pathways.
  
- **If the Hypothesis is Not Supported**: No significant difference in growth or metabolite profiles between glutamate-supplemented and control cells in the presence of spermine.

- **Independent Effects**: Glutamate supplementation alone (in the absence of spermine) may increase growth due to enhanced nitrogen metabolism, but this should be distinguished from effects observed in the presence of spermine.

This experimental design aims to isolate the effect of intracellular glutamate levels on spermine resistance while controlling for other variables.
