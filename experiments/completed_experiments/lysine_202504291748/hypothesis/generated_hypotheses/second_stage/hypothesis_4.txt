### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased hyperosmotic stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae when exposed to media containing 30% sucrose. To explore this association, we need to design an experiment that modulates intracellular lysine levels and evaluates the yeast's ability to resist hyperosmotic stress under these conditions.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will correlate with a reduction in hyperosmotic stress resistance when the cells are exposed to a media containing 30% (w/v) sucrose. The underlying mechanism may involve lysine's role in cellular osmoregulation or its effect on metabolic pathways influencing stress responses.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain. Begin with minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Lysine Supplementation**:
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: Supplement media with 5 mM lysine.
   - High Lysine: Supplement media with 20 mM lysine (supraphysiological level).

3. **Hyperosmotic Stress Condition**:
   - All experimental wells will contain media with 30% (w/v) sucrose to induce hyperosmotic stress.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Include a negative control with 20 mM valine (equimolar to the highest lysine concentration) instead of lysine to ensure that any observed effects are specific to lysine and not due to amino acid supplementation.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Prepare a 96-well plate with the following conditions:
     - Control + 30% sucrose
     - Low Lysine + 30% sucrose
     - High Lysine + 30% sucrose
     - Valine (Negative Control) + 30% sucrose
   - Each condition should be replicated across multiple wells to ensure statistical validity.

6. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.
   - At the end of the 20-hour experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine concentrations.

7. **Temperature and Shaking**:
   - Maintain cultures at 30°C with constant shaking to ensure aeration and homogeneity.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Condition**: Cells should exhibit a baseline level of hyperosmotic stress resistance.
- **Low Lysine Supplementation**: If lysine affects stress resistance, we might observe a slight decrease in growth or viability compared to the control.
- **High Lysine Supplementation**: A more pronounced decrease in hyperosmotic stress resistance is expected if the hypothesis is correct.
- **Negative Control (Valine)**: This condition should not significantly affect hyperosmotic stress resistance compared to the control, confirming that the effects observed in lysine-supplemented conditions are specific to lysine.

By comparing growth curves and intracellular lysine levels, we can determine whether higher lysine concentrations correlate with decreased hyperosmotic stress resistance, thereby supporting or refuting the initial statement.
