### Motivation
Proline is an amino acid that plays a critical role in stress responses, including resistance to various environmental stressors. The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular proline levels and decreased resistance to chitosan, a compound known to have antimicrobial properties. Understanding this association could provide insights into the metabolic pathways involved in stress resistance in S. cerevisiae.

### Hypothesis
Cells with reduced intracellular proline levels exhibit decreased resistance to chitosan due to proline's role in stabilizing cellular structures and protecting against oxidative stress. Lower proline levels may impair these protective mechanisms, leading to increased sensitivity to chitosan.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for baseline conditions.

2. **Proline Supplementation Levels**
   - Control: No proline supplementation.
   - Low Proline: Supplement with L-proline at 0.5 mM.
   - High Proline: Supplement with L-proline at 2 mM.

3. **Chitosan Treatment Levels**
   - Control: No chitosan treatment.
   - Chitosan Treatment: Add chitosan at 0.1% (w/v).

4. **Negative Control**
   - Supplement with 2 mM alanine (equimolar to the highest proline concentration) without chitosan treatment.

5. **Experimental Conditions**
   - Total conditions: 3 proline levels x 2 chitosan levels + 1 negative control = 7 conditions.
   - Each condition will be set up in triplicate in a 96-well plate.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Measurements**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth curves and assess viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.

7. **Assumptions Verification**
   - Verify that proline supplementation effectively increases intracellular proline levels.
   - Confirm that chitosan treatment affects cell viability as expected.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Proline Supplementation Effects**: Higher intracellular proline levels are expected to correlate with increased resistance to chitosan, reflected in more robust growth curves compared to control.
- **Chitosan Treatment Effects**: Chitosan is expected to reduce growth in all conditions, with the extent of reduction varying based on intracellular proline levels.
- **Negative Control**: Alanine supplementation should not significantly affect growth or chitosan resistance, confirming its role as a neutral control.
- **Overall**: If the hypothesis is correct, cells with low proline supplementation will show decreased resistance to chitosan, supporting the proposed association. Conversely, high proline supplementation should mitigate chitosan's effects, indicating proline's protective role.
