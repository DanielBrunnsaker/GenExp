### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid. Proline is known to act as an osmoprotectant and a stress protectant in various organisms, including yeast. By examining this association, we can understand whether proline supplementation can enhance resistance to (S)-lactic acid, which could be useful for improving yeast performance in industrial fermentation processes where lactic acid is a common byproduct.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will enhance resistance to (S)-lactic acid due to proline's role in stress protection and stabilization of cellular structures, potentially reducing the detrimental effects of lactic acid on cellular viability.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae. Grow the cells in minimal media without amino acids unless specified otherwise.

2. **Supplementation Levels**:
   - **Proline**: 0 mM (Control), 5 mM, 10 mM.
   - **Negative Control**: Alanine at 10 mM.

3. **Treatment**:
   - **(S)-Lactic Acid**: 0 mM (Control), 50 mM.

4. **Experimental Set-Up**:
   - Prepare 96-well plates with the following combinations:
     - Proline 0 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 0 mM
     - Proline 0 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 50 mM
     - Proline 5 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 0 mM
     - Proline 5 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 50 mM
     - Proline 10 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 0 mM
     - Proline 10 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 50 mM
     - Alanine 10 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 0 mM (Negative Control)
     - Alanine 10 mM + (S)-Lactic Acid 50 mM (Negative Control)

5. **Incubation**: Incubate at 30°C with shaking. Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

6. **Metabolomics**: At the end of 20 hours, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels to confirm successful modulation.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions**: Cells with 0 mM proline and no lactic acid should exhibit normal growth, serving as a baseline.
  
- **Proline Supplementation**: 
  - If increased proline enhances resistance, cells supplemented with 5 mM and 10 mM proline should show better growth/viability in the presence of 50 mM lactic acid compared to non-supplemented cells.
  - Proline-supplemented cells without lactic acid should grow similarly to control, indicating no negative impact of proline itself.

- **Negative Control**: Alanine supplementation should not confer increased resistance, validating its role as a control for amino acid supplementation effects.

- **Verification**: Intracellular proline measurements should confirm higher proline levels in supplemented conditions, supporting the hypothesis that proline accumulation contributes to lactic acid resistance.
