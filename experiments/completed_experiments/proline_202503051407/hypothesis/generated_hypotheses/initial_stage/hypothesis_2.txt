**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is known to play roles in stress protection, potentially acting as an osmoprotectant or stabilizing proteins. This experiment aims to determine if increasing intracellular proline levels enhances resistance to (S)-lactic acid.

**Hypothesis:**
Increasing intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae enhances the cells' resistance to (S)-lactic acid, potentially by stabilizing proteins and membranes or acting as an osmoprotectant under chemical stress.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids. 

2. **Proline Supplementation:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No proline supplementation.
   - Level 2: Supplement with 10 mM proline.
   - Level 3: Supplement with 20 mM proline.

3. **(S)-Lactic Acid Treatment:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No (S)-lactic acid.
   - Level 2: 50 mM (S)-lactic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use alanine as a negative control. Supplement with 20 mM alanine, equivalent to the highest proline concentration.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Set up all combinations of proline levels and (S)-lactic acid treatments, resulting in 6 conditions (3 proline levels x 2 lactic acid levels).
   - Include two additional conditions for the negative control with and without (S)-lactic acid.

6. **Measurement and Analysis:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline concentrations.
   - Compare growth curves and intracellular proline levels across conditions to evaluate the association between proline levels and lactic acid resistance.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Proline Supplementation Effects:** Increased intracellular proline levels (from supplementation) should correlate with higher resistance to (S)-lactic acid, observed as better growth or viability in treated conditions.
- **Negative Control:** Alanine supplementation should not confer increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid, confirming that the effect is specific to proline.
- **Independent Effects:** Proline may independently affect growth even in the absence of (S)-lactic acid, potentially enhancing growth due to its role in stress protection.
- **Verification:** Mass spectrometry should confirm increased intracellular proline levels correlating with the supplemented conditions.
