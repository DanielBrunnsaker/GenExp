### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and decreased metal resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae when exposed to indium chloride. Proline is known to play a role in stress responses, including osmotic and oxidative stress, but its specific impact on metal resistance, particularly indium, is not well understood. This experiment aims to explore whether increased intracellular proline levels correlate with decreased resistance to indium chloride, potentially providing insights into proline's role in metal stress responses.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will lead to decreased resistance to indium chloride, as indicated by reduced growth rates or viability in the presence of indium chloride. This may occur due to proline's potential interference with cellular metal detoxification pathways or stress response mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Base media: Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Supplementation:**
   - Proline supplementation at two levels:
     - Control: 0 mM proline.
     - Low: 2 mM proline.
     - High: 5 mM proline.

3. **Treatment:**
   - Indium chloride treatment at a concentration of 2.6 mM.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use alanine at 5 mM as a negative control, as it is not expected to impact metal resistance.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - 3 proline levels x 2 indium chloride conditions (treated and untreated) + 2 negative control conditions (alanine treated and untreated) = 8 total conditions.
   - Each condition should be tested in separate wells in a 96-well plate format.
   - All supplementations and treatments are added at t=0h.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At the end of the experiment (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.
   - Verify viability through growth curves.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Proline Levels:**
  - If increased proline levels lead to decreased indium resistance, we expect to see reduced growth rates or viability in the presence of indium chloride in the high proline supplementation condition compared to the control.
  
- **Negative Control:**
  - Alanine supplementation should not affect indium resistance, serving as a baseline for comparison.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - Proline supplementation may independently affect growth or stress responses, so any observed effects must be compared against the control conditions to isolate the impact of indium chloride treatment.

This experiment will help determine if there is a correlation between proline levels and indium resistance, contributing to a better understanding of proline's role in metal stress responses in yeast.
