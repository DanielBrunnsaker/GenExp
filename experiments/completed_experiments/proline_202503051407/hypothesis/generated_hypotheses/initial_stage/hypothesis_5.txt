### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to yttrium chloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is an amino acid known for its role in stress protection, potentially acting as an osmoprotectant or stabilizer of proteins and membranes. Investigating this association could provide insights into the protective mechanisms of proline against chemical stress.

### Hypothesis

Cells with increased intracellular proline concentrations exhibit enhanced resistance to yttrium chloride due to proline's role in stress response and stabilization of cellular structures.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Baseline Conditions**: Use minimal media without amino acids for all experiments.

2. **Proline Supplementation**:
   - Control: No proline supplementation.
   - Low Proline: 1 mM proline.
   - High Proline: 5 mM proline.

3. **Yttrium Chloride Treatment**:
   - Control: No yttrium chloride.
   - Treatment: 0.5 mM yttrium chloride.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use valine as the negative control at 5 mM, equimolar to the highest proline supplementation. Valine is chosen as it is an amino acid not directly involved in stress response pathways like proline.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Prepare 96-well plates with each well containing the appropriate media and supplementation.
   - Inoculate with a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Conditions**:
   - 3 levels of proline (0, 1, 5 mM) x 2 levels of yttrium chloride (0, 0.5 mM) = 6 conditions.
   - 2 additional wells for valine control (with and without yttrium chloride).

7. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline concentrations.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Resistance**: If proline enhances resistance, cells with higher proline supplementation should exhibit better growth and viability in the presence of yttrium chloride compared to those without proline supplementation.
  
- **No Effect**: If proline does not confer resistance, there will be no significant difference in growth between proline-supplemented and non-supplemented cells under yttrium chloride treatment.

- **Negative Control Validation**: Valine supplementation should not confer resistance to yttrium chloride, confirming its role as a suitable negative control.

This design allows us to evaluate the specific association between proline levels and chemical resistance, while controlling for potential confounders.
