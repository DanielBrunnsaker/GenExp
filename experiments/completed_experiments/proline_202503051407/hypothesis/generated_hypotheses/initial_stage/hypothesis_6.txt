**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and decreased metal resistance, potentially mediated through the inhibition of a target involved in citrate metabolism. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the role of proline in metal stress responses and metabolic regulation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

**Hypothesis:**
Increased intracellular proline levels inhibit a target involved in citrate metabolism, leading to decreased metal resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline may interfere with citrate metabolism pathways, affecting metal detoxification processes.

**Experimental Plan:**
1. **Base Media:** Use minimal media without amino acids to ensure that any observed effects are due to proline supplementation.

2. **Proline Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No proline supplementation.
   - Low Proline: Supplement with 2 mM proline.
   - High Proline: Supplement with 5 mM proline.

3. **Metal Treatment:**
   - Treat with 0.5 mM copper sulfate to test metal resistance. Copper is a common metal stressor in yeast studies.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Supplement with 5 mM alanine (an amino acid not involved in citrate metabolism) to confirm that observed effects are specific to proline.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Combine each proline level with the copper sulfate treatment, resulting in six conditions (3 proline levels x 1 copper treatment, plus the alanine control with and without copper).
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking to maintain aerobic conditions.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and collect cells for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline and citrate concentrations.

6. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Confirm that proline supplementation increases intracellular proline levels using metabolomics.
   - Verify that alanine does not affect citrate metabolism or metal resistance.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If the hypothesis is correct, higher intracellular proline levels (5 mM supplementation) should correlate with decreased growth in the presence of copper, indicating reduced metal resistance.
- Mass spectrometry should show altered citrate metabolism in high proline conditions, supporting the inhibition mechanism.
- The alanine control should show no significant change in metal resistance or citrate metabolism, confirming the specificity of proline's effect.
- If proline does not affect metal resistance or citrate metabolism, the hypothesis may need reevaluation, suggesting that other pathways or mechanisms are involved.
