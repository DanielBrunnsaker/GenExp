### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and decreased resistance to calcium dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is known for its role in stress protection, but its relationship with calcium ion resistance is not well established. This experiment aims to explore whether increased intracellular proline affects the yeast's resistance to calcium dichloride.

### Hypothesis
Higher intracellular levels of proline in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may lead to decreased resistance to calcium dichloride due to potential interference with calcium ion homeostasis or signaling pathways.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Proline Supplementation:**
   - Level 1: Control (no proline supplementation)
   - Level 2: 5 mM proline supplementation
   - Level 3: 10 mM proline supplementation

3. **Calcium Dichloride Treatment:**
   - Level 1: Control (no calcium dichloride treatment)
   - Level 2: 10 mM calcium dichloride treatment

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use asparagine at 10 mM as a negative control to test the specificity of proline's effect. Asparagine is chosen because it is not directly involved in calcium ion regulation.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Combination of proline levels with and without calcium dichloride treatment:
     - Control (No proline, No calcium dichloride)
     - 5 mM Proline, No calcium dichloride
     - 10 mM Proline, No calcium dichloride
     - No proline, 10 mM calcium dichloride
     - 5 mM Proline, 10 mM calcium dichloride
     - 10 mM Proline, 10 mM calcium dichloride
     - Asparagine control, No calcium dichloride
     - Asparagine control, 10 mM calcium dichloride

6. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Inoculate each condition in a 96-well plate with appropriate replicates.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to determine growth curves.
   - At the end of 20 hours, harvest cells for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions:** Cells without proline supplementation should exhibit normal growth and resistance to calcium dichloride.
- **Proline Supplementation without Calcium Dichloride:** Increased proline levels should not affect growth significantly under standard conditions.
- **Proline Supplementation with Calcium Dichloride:** If higher proline levels decrease resistance to calcium dichloride, cells with 5 mM and 10 mM proline should show reduced growth compared to controls.
- **Asparagine Control:** Should not affect resistance to calcium dichloride, confirming the specificity of proline's effect.
- **Metabolomics Analysis:** Confirm that intracellular proline levels increase with supplementation and correlate these levels with growth and resistance outcomes.

This experimental design will help determine whether the association between proline levels and decreased resistance to calcium dichloride exists and explore the underlying mechanisms.
