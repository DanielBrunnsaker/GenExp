### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is known to function as an osmoprotectant and stress protectant in yeast, which could contribute to chemical resistance. This experiment aims to investigate whether modulating intracellular proline levels affects resistance to (S)-lactic acid and to what extent.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae enhances resistance to (S)-lactic acid by stabilizing proteins and membranes, thus reducing the detrimental effects of lactic acid stress.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids, supplemented as specified below.

2. **Proline Supplementation:**
   - Prepare three levels of proline supplementation:
     - Control: No proline.
     - Low Proline: 2 mM proline.
     - High Proline: 5 mM proline.

3. **(S)-Lactic Acid Treatment:**
   - Prepare two levels of (S)-lactic acid treatment:
     - Control: No (S)-lactic acid.
     - Treatment: 20 mM (S)-lactic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use alanine as a negative control, provided at 5 mM, equimolar to the highest proline supplementation level.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Combine all proline supplementation levels with both (S)-lactic acid treatment levels (3 proline levels x 2 lactic acid levels = 6 conditions).
   - Include the negative control with and without (S)-lactic acid treatment (2 conditions).

6. **Culture Conditions:**
   - Inoculate cultures in 96-well plates, ensuring consistent initial OD600.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking for 20 hours.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

7. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of the experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.

8. **Verification:**
   - Ensure that increased proline supplementation leads to elevated intracellular proline levels through metabolomics analysis.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Proline Levels:** Cultures supplemented with proline should show increased intracellular proline levels, confirmed by metabolomics.
- **Growth and Viability:**
  - If higher intracellular proline levels confer resistance, cultures with proline supplementation should exhibit better growth and higher OD600 in the presence of (S)-lactic acid compared to controls.
  - The negative control (alanine) should show no significant resistance to (S)-lactic acid compared to unsupplemented controls.
- **Dose-Response Relationship:** A dose-dependent increase in resistance to (S)-lactic acid is expected with increasing proline supplementation, supporting the hypothesis of proline's protective role.
- **Independent Effects:** Any observed effect should be evaluated for its independence from proline supplementation alone, ensuring that (S)-lactic acid resistance is specifically enhanced by proline.
