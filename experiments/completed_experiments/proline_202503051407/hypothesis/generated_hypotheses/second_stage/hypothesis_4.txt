### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into stress resistance mechanisms and potential applications in industrial fermentation processes where lactic acid is a byproduct.

### Hypothesis

Elevated intracellular proline levels enhance the resistance of S. cerevisiae to (S)-lactic acid by stabilizing cellular structures or acting as an osmoprotectant, thereby mitigating the stress effects induced by lactic acid.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae. Grow the cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure control over supplementation.

2. **Proline Supplementation**:
   - Control: No proline supplementation.
   - Low Proline: 10 mM proline.
   - High Proline: 50 mM proline.

3. **Lactic Acid Treatment**:
   - Control: No lactic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM (S)-lactic acid.

4. **Negative Control**: Include a negative control with alanine at 50 mM, as alanine is not expected to specifically counteract the effects of lactic acid stress.

5. **Experimental Conditions**: 
   - Set up each combination of proline supplementation and lactic acid treatment in separate wells.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking to ensure homogenous growth conditions.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At the end of 20 hours, harvest cells for intracellular metabolomics using mass spectrometry to quantify proline levels.

6. **Verification of Assumptions**: Include conditions with only proline or only lactic acid to evaluate their independent effects on growth and metabolite accumulation.

7. **Total Conditions**: 
   - Proline Levels: 3 (Control, Low, High)
   - Lactic Acid Levels: 2 (Control, Treatment)
   - Negative Control: 2 (Treated, Untreated with alanine)
   - Total: 3 x 2 + 2 = 8 conditions

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Proline, No Lactic Acid**: Expect normal growth, with intracellular proline levels correlating with supplementation levels.
- **Increased Proline, With Lactic Acid**: If the hypothesis is correct, wells with higher proline should exhibit better growth and viability compared to the control, indicating increased resistance to lactic acid.
- **Control Proline, With Lactic Acid**: Expect reduced growth due to lactic acid stress, serving as a baseline for comparison.
- **Negative Control (Alanine)**: Should not show significant resistance to lactic acid, confirming that the observed effects are specific to proline.

This experimental design aims to elucidate the potential protective role of proline against lactic acid stress in S. cerevisiae, while accounting for independent effects of the treatments.
