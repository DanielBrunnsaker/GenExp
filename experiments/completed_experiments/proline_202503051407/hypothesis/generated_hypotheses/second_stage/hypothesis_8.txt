**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of proline and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is known to function as an osmoprotectant and stress protectant in yeast, which could potentially confer resistance to various stressors, including chemical stress from lactic acid. This experiment aims to determine whether increasing intracellular proline levels indeed correlates with increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid.

**Hypothesis:**
Increasing intracellular proline levels in S. cerevisiae will enhance the yeast's resistance to (S)-lactic acid by stabilizing cellular structures and mitigating stress-induced damage.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media as the base for all conditions, without amino acids unless specified otherwise.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Proline Supplementation: 
     - Control: No proline.
     - Low Proline: 5 mM proline.
     - High Proline: 10 mM proline.
   - Negative Control: Alanine at 10 mM (equimolar to the highest proline concentration) to control for amino acid supplementation effects.

3. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - For each proline level (including control), prepare two sets of conditions:
     - No (S)-lactic acid treatment.
     - Treatment with 50 mM (S)-lactic acid, a physiologically relevant concentration known to induce stress in yeast.

4. **Experimental Design:**
   - Total conditions: 3 proline levels x 2 lactic acid treatments + 2 alanine controls = 8 conditions.
   - Inoculate all wells with a reference/wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking to ensure aeration.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.

5. **Verification:**
   - Ensure that proline uptake is effective by measuring intracellular proline levels in each condition using mass spectrometry.
   - Include growth curves to assess viability and resistance to (S)-lactic acid.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If the hypothesis is correct, an increase in intracellular proline levels should correlate with increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid, reflected by higher OD600 values in the presence of lactic acid compared to controls.
- The alanine control should show no significant increase in resistance, confirming that the observed effect is specific to proline.
- Mass spectrometry should confirm higher intracellular proline concentrations in the supplemented conditions, verifying effective uptake.
- The results will help determine if proline supplementation can be used as a strategy to enhance chemical resistance in yeast, specifically against (S)-lactic acid.
