### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of proline and increased resistance to lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is known to act as an osmoprotectant and can be involved in stress responses, potentially contributing to chemical resistance. This experiment aims to evaluate whether increasing intracellular proline levels can enhance resistance to lactic acid.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will enhance the cells' resistance to (S)-lactic acid, as proline may help stabilize proteins and membranes under stress conditions induced by lactic acid.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Proline Supplementation:**
   - Three levels of proline supplementation: 
     - Control: No proline.
     - Low proline: 0.5 mM proline.
     - High proline: 1.0 mM proline.

3. **Lactic Acid Treatment:**
   - Two levels of (S)-lactic acid treatment:
     - Control: No lactic acid.
     - Treatment: 50 mM (S)-lactic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with alanine supplementation at 1.0 mM, as alanine does not have known effects on chemical resistance.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Each combination of proline and lactic acid levels will be tested, resulting in 6 conditions (3 proline levels x 2 lactic acid levels).
   - Include two additional conditions for the negative control: 
     - Alanine with no lactic acid.
     - Alanine with lactic acid.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At the end of the experiment (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.

7. **Verification:**
   - Ensure that proline supplementation effectively increases intracellular proline levels by comparing metabolomics data across different proline supplementations.

### Expected Outcomes

- If higher intracellular proline levels confer increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid, we would expect:
  - Improved growth (higher OD600) in conditions with lactic acid when proline is supplemented, especially at 1.0 mM.
  - No significant growth improvement in the alanine control with lactic acid, indicating the effect is specific to proline.
- If the hypothesis is supported, the metabolomics data should show increased intracellular proline levels correlating with increased resistance.
- If no association is observed, growth rates in lactic acid conditions will not significantly differ with proline supplementation, and the negative control will exhibit similar growth patterns.
