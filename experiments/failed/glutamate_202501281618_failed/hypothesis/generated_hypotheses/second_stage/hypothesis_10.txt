### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of glutamate and decreased resistance to the chemical spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Spermine is a polyamine that can affect cell growth and viability. Investigating this association requires modulating intracellular glutamate levels and observing the resulting impact on resistance to spermine. This experiment will help discern whether elevated glutamate levels correlate with decreased spermine resistance, contributing to our understanding of yeast stress responses.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular glutamate levels will exhibit decreased resistance to spermine, potentially due to altered metabolic fluxes or stress response pathways influenced by glutamate. Glutamate may impact pathways involved in polyamine metabolism or cellular stress responses, thereby modulating the cell's ability to withstand spermine toxicity.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
   - Conduct experiments at 30°C with shaking to ensure optimal yeast growth.

2. **Supplementation:**
   - **Glutamate Supplementation:**
     - Control: No glutamate added.
     - Low Glutamate: Add 5 mM glutamate.
     - High Glutamate: Add 10 mM glutamate.
   - **Negative Control:**
     - Add 10 mM alanine (an amino acid not directly involved in glutamate metabolism) to a separate set of wells to serve as a negative control.

3. **Spermine Treatment:**
   - Prepare a 96-well plate with separate wells for each glutamate supplementation level.
   - Add spermine to each well at a concentration of 0.5 mM, a dose known to affect yeast viability.

4. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth curves.
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels and other relevant metabolites.

5. **Assumption Verification:**
   - Verify that glutamate supplementation effectively increases intracellular glutamate levels without significantly affecting growth in the absence of spermine.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Glutamate Levels:**
  - If increased intracellular glutamate levels are associated with decreased spermine resistance, we expect to see reduced growth or viability in the wells supplemented with higher glutamate concentrations compared to the control when exposed to spermine.
  
- **Negative Control:**
  - The alanine control should not show decreased resistance to spermine, confirming that any observed effects are specific to glutamate.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - Glutamate supplementation may independently affect growth or metabolic profiles. This effect should be accounted for by comparing growth and metabolomics data between the control and glutamate-supplemented conditions without spermine.

This experimental design aims to clarify the relationship between intracellular glutamate levels and resistance to spermine, providing insights into the metabolic and stress response pathways in yeast.
