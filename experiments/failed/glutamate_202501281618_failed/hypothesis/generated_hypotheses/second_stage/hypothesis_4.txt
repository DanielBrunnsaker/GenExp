### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of glutamate and decreased resistance to the chemical spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. To investigate this, we need to design an experiment that modulates intracellular glutamate levels and assesses the yeast's resistance to spermine. The goal is to determine whether changes in intracellular glutamate concentration correlate with changes in resistance to spermine.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular glutamate levels will exhibit decreased resistance to spermine due to a possible mechanism where higher glutamate concentrations affect cellular pathways that modulate spermine toxicity. Glutamate might influence polyamine metabolism or membrane integrity, which could alter the cell's ability to withstand spermine-induced stress.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.

2. **Glutamate Supplementation:**
   - Prepare three groups with different levels of glutamate supplementation:
     - Control: No glutamate supplementation.
     - Low Glutamate: Supplement with 1 mM glutamate.
     - High Glutamate: Supplement with 10 mM glutamate.

3. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control group with 10 mM alanine supplementation to ensure that any observed effects are specific to glutamate and not due to general amino acid supplementation.

4. **Spermine Treatment:**
   - At t=0h, add spermine to each well at a concentration of 1 mM, which is within the physiological range known to affect yeast cells.

5. **Measurements:**
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability over the 20-hour period.
   - At the end of the experiment, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels and assess any correlations with growth data.

6. **Temperature and Conditions:**
   - Maintain all cultures at 30°C with appropriate shaking to ensure aeration.

7. **Assumption Testing:**
   - Verify that glutamate supplementation effectively increases intracellular glutamate levels by comparing metabolomics data across different supplementation levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Glutamate Levels:**
  - If higher intracellular glutamate is associated with decreased resistance to spermine, we expect to see a reduced growth rate or increased cell death in the high glutamate group compared to the control when exposed to spermine.
  
- **Negative Control:**
  - The alanine-supplemented group should not show a significant change in resistance to spermine compared to the control, confirming that effects are specific to glutamate.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - It is possible that glutamate supplementation may independently affect cell growth or metabolism, which should be accounted for in the analysis. This includes potential effects on cellular pathways unrelated to spermine resistance.

This experimental design will allow us to assess whether there is an association between intracellular glutamate levels and resistance to spermine, supporting or refuting the initial statement.
