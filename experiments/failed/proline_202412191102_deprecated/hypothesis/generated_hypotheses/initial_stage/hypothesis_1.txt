**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship can provide insights into stress response mechanisms and potential metabolic adaptations in yeast.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with elevated intracellular proline levels exhibit increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid due to proline's role as an osmoprotectant and its involvement in stress response pathways. Proline may stabilize proteins and membranes or act as a free radical scavenger, thereby enhancing cell survival under lactic acid stress.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow all cultures in minimal media without amino acids.
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.

2. **Treatment Groups:**
   - **Control Group:** No additional supplementation.
   - **Proline Supplementation:**
     - Low Proline: Add 2 mM proline.
     - High Proline: Add 10 mM proline.
   - These concentrations are chosen based on known physiological relevance and their ability to modulate intracellular proline levels.

3. **Chemical Stress Application:**
   - Treat all groups with (S)-lactic acid at a concentration of 50 mM, a physiologically relevant stress-inducing concentration for yeast.

4. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.
   - At the end of the experiment (14 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels and confirm the modulation achieved by supplementation.

5. **Controls and Assumptions:**
   - Include a set of wells without (S)-lactic acid treatment to control for the independent effects of proline supplementation on growth.
   - Assume that proline supplementation effectively increases intracellular proline levels without causing significant off-target effects.

6. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Conduct a parallel set of experiments without (S)-lactic acid to evaluate the independent effects of proline on growth and metabolism.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Increased Resistance:** If higher intracellular proline levels confer increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid, the proline-supplemented groups (especially the high proline group) should show better growth (higher OD600) compared to the control group under lactic acid stress.
- **Independent Effect of Proline:** In the absence of (S)-lactic acid, proline supplementation may still affect growth rates due to its role in metabolism, but this effect should be less pronounced than under stress conditions.
- **Metabolomics Data:** Should show a dose-dependent increase in intracellular proline levels with supplementation, confirming that the observed phenotypic changes are associated with altered proline concentrations.

This experimental setup will help determine whether the association between proline levels and lactic acid resistance is supported by experimental data, while controlling for the independent effects of proline supplementation.
