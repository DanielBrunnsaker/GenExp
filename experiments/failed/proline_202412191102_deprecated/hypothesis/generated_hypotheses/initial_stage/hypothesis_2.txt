**Motivation**

The statement suggests that lower levels of intracellular proline in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may be associated with increased resistance to the chemical 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol. Proline is an amino acid that can influence various stress responses in yeast. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into how modulating proline levels affects chemical resistance, potentially informing strategies for industrial applications where yeast is exposed to various chemical stresses.

**Hypothesis**

Cells with reduced intracellular proline levels exhibit increased resistance to 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol due to a potential shift in stress response pathways. The hypothesis is that lower proline levels might alter cellular metabolism or signaling pathways that enhance the yeast's ability to withstand chemical stress.

**Experimental Plan**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for all experimental conditions.
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.

2. **Proline Modulation:**
   - Use L-proline supplementation to modulate intracellular proline levels. The aim is to create three conditions: low, medium, and high intracellular proline levels.
   - Supplementation Levels:
     - Control (no proline added).
     - Low proline: 0.1 mM L-proline.
     - High proline: 1.0 mM L-proline.
   - Justification: Proline supplementation is a direct method to modulate its intracellular concentration, with known effects on stress response and metabolism in yeast.

3. **Chemical Resistance Assay:**
   - At t=0h, add 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol to each well at a concentration of 50 µM, a physiologically relevant concentration for stress assays.
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes for 14 hours to assess growth and resistance.

4. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of the experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels and other metabolic changes.

5. **Control Experiments:**
   - Run parallel experiments without 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol to assess the independent effect of proline supplementation on growth and metabolism.

**Expected Outcomes**

- If the hypothesis is correct, cells with low intracellular proline levels (control or low proline supplementation) will exhibit higher growth rates and greater resistance to 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol compared to cells with high proline levels.
- Metabolomics should confirm lower intracellular proline levels in the control and low supplementation conditions.
- Independent effects of proline supplementation might include altered growth rates or stress responses, which will be assessed by the control experiments without the chemical.
- The results will help determine if the association between low proline levels and increased chemical resistance is supported or refuted by the experimental data.
