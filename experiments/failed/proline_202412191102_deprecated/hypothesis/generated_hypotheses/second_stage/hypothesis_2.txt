**Motivation:**
The statement suggests that Saccharomyces cerevisiae cells with higher intracellular proline levels may exhibit increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid. This experiment aims to explore the association between proline accumulation and chemical resistance, focusing on (S)-lactic acid, under standard growth conditions.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with elevated intracellular proline levels will exhibit increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid due to proline's role in stress response mechanisms. Proline might act as an osmoprotectant or radical scavenger, enhancing cell survival under lactic acid stress.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for all wells.

2. **Proline Supplementation:**
   - Design three treatment levels for proline supplementation:
     - Control: 0 mM proline (no supplementation).
     - Low proline: 5 mM proline.
     - High proline: 10 mM proline.
   - Justification: Proline is known to modulate stress responses in yeast, and these concentrations are within the range that can affect intracellular levels without causing growth inhibition.

3. **(S)-Lactic Acid Treatment:**
   - After inoculating the cultures at 0.05 OD600, add (S)-lactic acid to each well to a final concentration of 50 mM to simulate stress conditions.
   - Maintain a control set without lactic acid for each proline level to account for the independent effects of proline.

4. **Growth Monitoring:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth rates and assess viability over the 14-hour period.

5. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of the experiment (14 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels and verify that supplementation effectively modulated these levels.

6. **Control and Assumption Testing:**
   - Include wells with only (S)-lactic acid (no proline) to confirm its impact on growth.
   - Include a set of wells with only proline (no lactic acid) to evaluate its independent effects on growth and metabolism.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If the hypothesis is correct, cells supplemented with higher proline levels should exhibit better growth rates and higher viability under (S)-lactic acid stress compared to the control.
- The metabolomics analysis should confirm increased intracellular proline levels in supplemented groups, correlating with improved resistance.
- Proline alone (without lactic acid) may show modest effects on growth, but significant differences in resistance should only be observed under lactic acid stress.
- This experiment will help determine if proline supplementation is sufficient to enhance resistance to (S)-lactic acid, supporting or refuting the proposed association.
