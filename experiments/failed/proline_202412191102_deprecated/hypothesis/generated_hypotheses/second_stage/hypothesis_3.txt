### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association could provide insights into the protective mechanisms that proline might confer against chemical stressors, such as lactic acid, which is relevant in industrial fermentation processes.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular proline levels exhibit increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid due to proline's role as an osmoprotectant and its ability to stabilize proteins and membranes under stress conditions. This hypothesis will be tested by modulating proline levels and observing the growth and viability of yeast cells in the presence of (S)-lactic acid.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a 96-well plate format with three different conditions to modulate intracellular proline levels:
     - **Control:** Minimal media without amino acids.
     - **Low Proline Supplementation:** Minimal media supplemented with 0.5 mM proline.
     - **High Proline Supplementation:** Minimal media supplemented with 5 mM proline.
   - All cultures will be inoculated at an initial OD600 of 0.05.

2. **Chemical Stress Treatment:**
   - Introduce (S)-lactic acid at a concentration of 50 mM to all wells at the start of the experiment (t=0h). This concentration is chosen based on its relevance to industrial fermentation stress conditions.

3. **Controls:**
   - Include wells without (S)-lactic acid for each proline condition to assess the independent effect of proline on growth and viability.

4. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth rates.
   - At the end of 14 hours, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to quantify intracellular proline levels.

5. **Assumptions Verification:**
   - Include a separate set of wells with only proline supplementation (without (S)-lactic acid) to verify that proline supplementation does not independently affect growth rates under non-stress conditions.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Condition:** Cells are expected to exhibit standard growth rates with reduced viability in the presence of (S)-lactic acid due to the lack of osmoprotective compounds.
- **Low Proline Supplementation:** Cells may show slightly improved resistance to (S)-lactic acid compared to the control, with moderate growth rates and viability.
- **High Proline Supplementation:** Cells are expected to demonstrate the highest resistance to (S)-lactic acid, with increased growth rates and viability, supporting the hypothesis that elevated proline levels confer protective effects.
- **Verification of Assumptions:** Proline supplementation in the absence of (S)-lactic acid should not significantly affect growth rates, confirming that observed effects under stress conditions are due to the interaction between proline and (S)-lactic acid stress.

This experimental design will allow us to assess whether intracellular proline levels are indeed associated with increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid, thus supporting or refuting the initial statement.
