### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is an amino acid known to play roles in stress resistance, possibly by acting as an osmoprotectant or stabilizing proteins and membranes. Testing this association requires an experiment that modulates intracellular proline levels and assesses resistance to (S)-lactic acid.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular proline concentrations will exhibit increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid. The potential mechanism is that proline acts as an osmoprotectant, stabilizing cellular structures and reducing the detrimental effects of (S)-lactic acid on cellular integrity and function.

### Experimental Plan

#### Setup:

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Grow cells in minimal media (without amino acids) to ensure baseline conditions without external proline.

2. **Proline Modulation**: 
   - Control: No supplementation (baseline proline levels).
   - Low Proline: Supplement with 0.5 mM proline to moderately increase intracellular proline.
   - High Proline: Supplement with 2.0 mM proline to achieve a significant increase in intracellular proline.

3. **Chemical Resistance Testing**:
   - Prepare (S)-lactic acid treatments at different concentrations to test resistance: 0 mM (control), 50 mM, and 100 mM. These concentrations are chosen based on typical stress levels that affect yeast growth.

4. **Experimental Design**:
   - Inoculate each well with cells at an initial OD600 of 0.05.
   - Add proline supplementation at t=0h as per the treatment levels.
   - Introduce (S)-lactic acid concentrations at t=0h.
   - Incubate cultures with shaking at 30°C for 14 hours.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to track growth and assess viability.

5. **Metabolomics Analysis**:
   - At the end of the experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels and verify the effectiveness of proline supplementation.

#### Controls and Assumptions:

- **Negative Control**: Cells with no proline supplementation and no (S)-lactic acid exposure.
- **Positive Control**: Cells with no proline supplementation but exposed to 100 mM (S)-lactic acid to observe the baseline stress response.
- **Assumption Verification**: Ensure that proline supplementation does not independently affect cell growth by comparing growth rates in the absence of (S)-lactic acid.

- **Increased Resistance**: If the hypothesis is correct, cells with higher intracellular proline levels (2.0 mM supplementation) should show higher OD600 values (indicating better growth) compared to controls when exposed to (S)-lactic acid.
