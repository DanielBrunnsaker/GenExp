Selected Statement: 
```prolog
Cell(A):-phenotype(A,'increased resistance to chemicals',B,C),compound_name(B,'derivative myriocin').
```

Motivation: 
The selected statement, involving increased resistance to a chemical (derivative myriocin) associated with higher intracellular glutamate levels, presents an intriguing hypothesis due to its implication of potential protective effects of glutamate. This is novel compared to the other statements, which suggest decreased resistance. Investigating increased resistance is both scientifically interesting and potentially impactful for understanding cellular defense mechanisms.

Hypothesis:
Cells with higher than normal intracellular glutamate levels exhibit increased resistance to derivative myriocin compared to wild-type cells with normal glutamate levels.

Experimental Plan:

1. **Objective**: To test if elevating intracellular glutamate levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae increases resistance to derivative myriocin.

2. **Cultures**: Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain. Grow cultures in 96-well plates, with each well containing 200 µL of minimal media without amino acids. Inoculate all cultures to an initial OD600 of 0.05.

3. **Supplementation**:
   - **L-Glutamate**: To modulate intracellular glutamate levels, supplement media with:
     - Control: No L-Glutamate
     - Low: 5 mM L-Glutamate
     - High: 10 mM L-Glutamate
   
   - **Derivative Myriocin Treatment**: To test resistance:
     - Control: No myriocin
     - Low: 0.5 µg/mL myriocin
     - High: 1.0 µg/mL myriocin

4. **Assumptions**:
   - Increased extracellular L-Glutamate will proportionally increase intracellular glutamate levels.
   - Derivative myriocin concentrations chosen are physiologically relevant for testing resistance.

5. **Controls**:
   - Each glutamate level will be tested without myriocin to observe the independent effect of glutamate on growth.
   - Each myriocin level will be tested without glutamate supplementation to establish baseline resistance.

6. **Measurements**:
   - **Growth Curve**: Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for 12 hours.
   - **Metabolomics**: At 12 hours, use mass-spectrometry to measure intracellular glutamate levels.

7. **Temperature**: Incubate all cultures at 30°C with shaking.

Expected Outcomes:

1. **Hypothesis Testing**:
   - If cells supplemented with L-Glutamate (5 mM or 10 mM) show higher OD600 in the presence of myriocin compared to controls, this would support the hypothesis that increased intracellular glutamate levels confer increased resistance to derivative myriocin.
   
2. **Independent Effects**:
   - **Glutamate Alone**: Increased glutamate levels (without myriocin) may lead to altered growth rates. Compare to control to isolate glutamate's effect.
   - **Myriocin Alone**: Establish baseline growth inhibition at 0.5 µg/mL and 1.0 µg/mL without glutamate supplementation.

3. **Verification of Assumptions**:
   - Mass spectrometry results confirming elevated intracellular glutamate levels when supplemented externally validate the assumption about glutamate modulation.

By testing these hypotheses and outcomes, the designed experiment can effectively probe the association between intracellular glutamate levels and resistance to derivative myriocin in yeast cells.
