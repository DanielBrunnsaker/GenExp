**Selected Statement:**
```prolog
Cell(A):-phenotype(A,'decreased resistance to chemicals',B,C),compound_name(B,'formic acid').
```

**Motivation:**
The selected statement investigates the association between elevated levels of intracellular glutamate and decreased resistance to formic acid. Formic acid is a simple carboxylic acid that can affect cellular metabolism and viability. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into cellular stress responses and glutamate's role in modulating them. This statement presents a feasible hypothesis to test using a controlled experimental setup, and the effects of formic acid are well-documented, making it a suitable candidate for this study.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with higher than normal levels of intracellular glutamate will exhibit decreased resistance to formic acid compared to cells with normal glutamate levels when grown in minimal media without amino acids.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for baseline conditions.

2. **Glutamate Modulation:**
   - Supplement the media with L-glutamate to create three conditions:
     - Control: 0 mM L-glutamate (baseline)
     - Moderate: 5 mM L-glutamate
     - High: 10 mM L-glutamate

3. **Formic Acid Treatment:**
   - At the start of the experiment (t=0h), introduce formic acid at three levels:
     - Low: 10 mM formic acid
     - Moderate: 20 mM formic acid
     - High: 30 mM formic acid

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Inoculate cultures at 0.05 OD600 in 96-well plates.
   - Each well corresponds to a unique combination of glutamate and formic acid concentrations.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for 12 hours to monitor growth.

5. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At experiment termination, perform mass-spectrometry based metabolomics to confirm intracellular glutamate levels.

6. **Control Experiments:**
   - Verify that L-glutamate supplementation effectively increases intracellular glutamate without pleiotropic effects by comparing metabolomics profiles of supplemented vs. non-supplemented cells.
   - Conduct a parallel set of experiments without formic acid to assess the independent effect of glutamate supplementation on growth.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Hypothesis Support:**
  - If cells with elevated glutamate levels show reduced growth or viability in the presence of formic acid compared to the control, the hypothesis is supported.
  - Metabolomics should confirm that intracellular glutamate levels are higher in supplemented conditions.

- **Hypothesis Refutation:**
  - If no significant difference in resistance to formic acid is observed across glutamate levels, the hypothesis is refuted.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - Glutamate supplementation alone should not significantly affect growth in the absence of formic acid, confirming that observed effects are due to the interaction with formic acid.

This experimental design comprehensively tests the association between intracellular glutamate levels and formic acid resistance, accounting for potential confounders and verifying assumptions.
