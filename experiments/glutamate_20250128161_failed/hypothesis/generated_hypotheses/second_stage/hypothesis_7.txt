### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between elevated intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to the chemical spermine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. To test this association, we need to design an experiment that modulates intracellular glutamate levels and assesses the yeast's resistance to spermine. This will help determine whether increased glutamate correlates with the phenotype described.

### Hypothesis

Cells with increased intracellular glutamate levels will demonstrate decreased resistance to spermine, potentially due to glutamate's role as a key metabolic intermediate and its influence on cellular stress responses. This may involve glutamate's participation in nitrogen metabolism and its effect on polyamine transport or metabolism, which could modulate spermine toxicity.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain Selection**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.

2. **Media Preparation**: 
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the base medium for all conditions.

3. **Supplementation**:
   - **Glutamate Supplementation**: Use three levels of glutamate to modulate intracellular glutamate levels:
     - Control: No glutamate supplementation.
     - Low: 1 mM glutamate.
     - High: 5 mM glutamate.
   - These concentrations are chosen based on their ability to modulate intracellular levels without causing growth inhibition.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use alanine at 5 mM as a negative control to ensure that any observed effects are specific to glutamate and not due to general amino acid supplementation.

5. **Spermine Treatment**:
   - Add spermine to the media at a concentration of 0.5 mM, a level known to affect yeast cells, to all wells at t=0h.

6. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Incubate all cultures at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.

7. **Measurements**:
   - At experiment termination, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels and other relevant metabolites.
   - Assess growth curves to determine resistance to spermine.

8. **Assumptions Testing**:
   - Verify that glutamate supplementation effectively increases intracellular glutamate without affecting other metabolic pathways independently.

### Expected Outcomes

- If the hypothesis is correct, cells supplemented with higher levels of glutamate should show decreased growth or viability in the presence of spermine compared to controls.
- The alanine control should not show significant differences in spermine resistance, confirming that the effect is specific to glutamate.
- Independent effects of glutamate supplementation, such as changes in growth rate or metabolism, should be accounted for in the analysis to isolate the association with spermine resistance.

This experimental design will help to elucidate the relationship between intracellular glutamate levels and spermine resistance, supporting or refuting the proposed association.
