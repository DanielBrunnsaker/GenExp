**Motivation:**
The statement suggests that lower intracellular levels of glutamine are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association can provide insights into the metabolic pathways and stress response mechanisms that are influenced by glutamine levels.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with reduced intracellular glutamine levels will show decreased growth and viability in the presence of acetic acid due to glutamine's role in stress response and cellular metabolism. This may be due to glutamine's involvement in nitrogen metabolism and its role as a precursor for the synthesis of nucleotides and other amino acids, which are crucial for cellular stress responses.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No glutamine supplementation.
   - Low Glutamine: 0.1 mM glutamine.
   - High Glutamine: 10 mM glutamine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Acetic Acid Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use leucine at 10 mM as a negative control to ensure that the observed effects are specific to glutamine modulation.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Each well will contain one of the following combinations: 
     - Control (no glutamine, no acetic acid)
     - Low Glutamine (0.1 mM), no acetic acid
     - High Glutamine (10 mM), no acetic acid
     - Control (no glutamine), with acetic acid (50 mM)
     - Low Glutamine (0.1 mM), with acetic acid (50 mM)
     - High Glutamine (10 mM), with acetic acid (50 mM)
     - Negative Control (10 mM leucine), no acetic acid
     - Negative Control (10 mM leucine), with acetic acid (50 mM)

6. **Procedure:**
   - Inoculate each well with a standardized concentration of the reference prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics at experiment termination to measure intracellular glutamine levels.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Control Conditions:** Expect normal growth and viability.
- **Low Glutamine, No Acetic Acid:** Reduced growth compared to control, confirming the effect of low glutamine.
- **High Glutamine, No Acetic Acid:** Similar or enhanced growth compared to control, indicating no adverse effect of high glutamine.
- **Control with Acetic Acid:** Reduced growth and viability due to acetic acid stress.
- **Low Glutamine with Acetic Acid:** Further reduced growth and viability compared to acetic acid alone, supporting the hypothesis that low glutamine decreases resistance to acetic acid.
- **High Glutamine with Acetic Acid:** Potentially improved growth compared to acetic acid alone, suggesting a protective role of high glutamine levels.
- **Negative Control (Leucine):** No significant change in growth with or without acetic acid, confirming that the effects observed are specific to glutamine modulation.

This experimental setup will help determine if lower glutamine levels are indeed associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid, supporting or refuting the statement.
