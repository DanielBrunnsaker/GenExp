### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could help elucidate the role of glutamine in stress resistance mechanisms and potentially identify metabolic targets for improving yeast tolerance to acetic acid, a common stressor in industrial fermentation processes.

### Hypothesis

Cells with reduced intracellular glutamine levels will exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid due to impaired nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways. Glutamine is a key nitrogen donor in various biosynthetic processes, and its depletion may compromise the cell's ability to detoxify acetic acid or maintain cellular homeostasis under stress.

### Experimental Plan

#### Conditions and Supplementations

1. **Control (No Glutamine Supplementation):**
   - Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Glutamine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Low: 1 mM glutamine.
   - High: 10 mM glutamine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Leucine supplementation at 10 mM (equimolar to the highest glutamine supplementation).

#### Experimental Setup

- **Media Preparation:** Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the base for all conditions.
- **Supplementation:** Add glutamine and leucine at specified concentrations at the start of the experiment (t=0h).
- **Acetic Acid Treatment:** Add acetic acid to designated wells at the start of the experiment.
- **Growth Monitoring:** Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
- **Metabolomics Analysis:** At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels.

#### Experimental Conditions

- 3 Glutamine levels (Control, Low, High) x 2 Acetic Acid treatments (Control, Treatment) = 6 conditions.
- Include treated and untreated negative control with leucine = 2 additional conditions.
- Total conditions = 8.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions:** Cells without glutamine supplementation are expected to exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid, supporting the association between low glutamine levels and acetic acid sensitivity.
- **Glutamine Supplementation:** Increasing glutamine levels should enhance resistance to acetic acid, indicating a protective role of glutamine in stress response.
- **Negative Control (Leucine):** Leucine supplementation should not affect acetic acid resistance, confirming that observed effects are specific to glutamine.
- **Metabolomics Verification:** Intracellular glutamine levels should correlate with the supplementation levels, validating the modulation approach.

By analyzing growth curves and intracellular metabolite levels, we can determine if glutamine supplementation mitigates the decreased resistance to acetic acid, thereby supporting or refuting the initial statement.
