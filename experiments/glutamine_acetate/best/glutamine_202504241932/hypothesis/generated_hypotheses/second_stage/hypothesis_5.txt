### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular levels of glutamine and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into metabolic pathways influencing stress resistance and may highlight potential targets for improving yeast robustness in industrial applications.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular glutamine levels reduce the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to acetic acid, potentially due to glutamine's role in nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways. By modulating glutamine levels, we can observe changes in acetic acid resistance, as measured by growth and viability.

### Experimental Plan

**Media and Conditions:**
- Use minimal media for all experiments.
- All experiments are conducted at 30°C with continuous shaking.
- Experiment duration is 20 hours.

**Supplementation Levels:**
1. **Glutamine Supplementation:**
   - Control: No glutamine added.
   - Physiological concentration: 2 mM glutamine.
   - Supraphysiological concentration: 10 mM glutamine.
   
2. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid (a concentration known to stress yeast cells).

**Negative Control:**
- Use threonine at 10 mM as a negative control to ensure that observed effects are specific to glutamine. Threonine is chosen because it is not directly involved in the pathways associated with acetic acid resistance.

**Experimental Conditions:**
- Total combinations: 3 glutamine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + 2 negative control conditions = 8 conditions.

**Steps:**
1. Prepare 96-well plates with minimal media.
2. Add respective glutamine concentrations to each well.
3. Add acetic acid to designated wells at t=0h.
4. Include wells with threonine as negative controls.
5. Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
6. At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels.

**Verification:**
- Ensure that the modulation of glutamine levels is achieved by measuring intracellular concentrations at the end of the experiment.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control (No acetic acid):** Growth should be observed across all glutamine levels, with potentially enhanced growth at higher glutamine concentrations due to improved nitrogen availability.
  
- **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
  - **No glutamine:** Expect reduced growth and viability, indicating decreased resistance to acetic acid.
  - **2 mM glutamine:** Partial restoration of growth, suggesting some protective effect of physiological glutamine levels.
  - **10 mM glutamine:** Further improvement in growth and viability, indicating enhanced resistance due to supraphysiological glutamine levels.

- **Negative Control (Threonine):** Growth should not be significantly affected by threonine supplementation, confirming that observed effects are specific to glutamine.

This experimental design aims to elucidate the role of glutamine in modulating resistance to acetic acid, providing a clearer understanding of its protective mechanisms in yeast cells.
