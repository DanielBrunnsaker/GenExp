**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship is important because glutamine is a key metabolite involved in numerous cellular processes, including nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways. By elucidating this association, we can better understand the metabolic adaptations of yeast to chemical stressors.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with reduced intracellular glutamine levels exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid due to impaired nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways, potentially affecting cellular detoxification processes and membrane integrity.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the basal condition.

2. **Glutamine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No glutamine supplementation.
   - Low Glutamine: Supplement with 1 mM glutamine.
   - High Glutamine: Supplement with 10 mM glutamine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use leucine at 10 mM as a negative control. This concentration is equimolar to the highest glutamine supplementation level. Leucine is relevant as it does not directly affect the pathways associated with acetic acid resistance.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Set up 96-well plates with each combination of glutamine supplementation and acetic acid treatment, including controls.
   - Conditions: 3 glutamine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + 2 negative controls = 8 total conditions.
   - Start all treatments at t=0h and incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels and other relevant metabolites.

7. **Assumptions Verification:**
   - Include experiments to verify that glutamine supplementation modulates intracellular levels as expected.
   - Ensure acetic acid concentration is physiologically relevant and does not cause complete cell death in all conditions.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If the hypothesis is correct, cells with lower intracellular glutamine (control and low glutamine conditions) will show decreased growth and viability in the presence of acetic acid compared to high glutamine supplementation.
- Mass spectrometry should confirm lower intracellular glutamine levels in the control and low glutamine conditions.
- The negative control (leucine) should not significantly alter resistance to acetic acid, confirming specificity of the glutamine effect.
- A dose-response relationship may be observed, with higher glutamine levels correlating with increased resistance to acetic acid, supporting the association.
