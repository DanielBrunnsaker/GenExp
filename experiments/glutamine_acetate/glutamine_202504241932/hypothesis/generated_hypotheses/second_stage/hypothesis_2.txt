### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Glutamine is a critical amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways, including nitrogen metabolism, and may play a role in stress responses. Understanding this association could provide insights into the metabolic adaptations of yeast to chemical stressors such as acetic acid.

### Hypothesis
Lower intracellular glutamine levels result in decreased resistance to acetic acid due to impaired nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

### Experimental Plan
- **Strain**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
- **Media**: Minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
- **Supplementation**: Modulate intracellular glutamine levels using external glutamine supplementation.
  - **Levels**:
    1. Control: No glutamine supplementation.
    2. Physiological Level: 2 mM glutamine.
    3. Supraphysiological Level: 10 mM glutamine.
- **Treatment**: Acetic acid exposure.
  - **Levels**:
    1. Control: No acetic acid.
    2. Treatment: 20 mM acetic acid.
- **Negative Control**: Use leucine at 10 mM as a negative control for supplementation, since leucine is not directly involved in the pathways affected by acetic acid stress.
- **Experimental Conditions**: 
  - Set up 12 conditions (3 glutamine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + negative control with and without acetic acid).
  - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
  - Add all supplementations and treatments at t=0h.
  - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth.
  - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
  - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels at experiment termination.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Control Conditions**: Cells without acetic acid should exhibit normal growth regardless of glutamine supplementation, allowing for the assessment of glutamine's independent effects.
- **Glutamine Supplementation**:
  - **No Acetic Acid**: Higher glutamine levels may enhance growth slightly due to improved nitrogen metabolism.
  - **With Acetic Acid**: 
    - **Low Glutamine (Control)**: Expect decreased growth and viability, indicating reduced resistance.
    - **Physiological and Supraphysiological Glutamine**: Potentially improved resistance to acetic acid, observed as better growth and higher viability compared to the control.
- **Negative Control (Leucine)**: Should not affect resistance to acetic acid, confirming the specificity of the glutamine-acetic acid interaction.

This experimental setup aims to test whether modulating intracellular glutamine levels influences the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to acetic acid, thereby supporting or refuting the association mentioned in the statement.
