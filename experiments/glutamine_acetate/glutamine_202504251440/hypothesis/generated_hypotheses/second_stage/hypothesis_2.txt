**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between lower levels of intracellular glutamine and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Acetic acid is a common stressor in yeast, affecting cell viability and growth. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into metabolic adaptations and stress responses in yeast.

**Hypothesis:**
Lower intracellular glutamine levels reduce the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to acetic acid by impairing the synthesis of key stress response proteins or metabolites that are glutamine-dependent.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Baseline Conditions:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids.
   - Maintain standard shaking and inoculation conditions.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - **Glutamine Supplementation:**
     - Control (0 mM glutamine)
     - Physiological level (1 mM glutamine)
     - Supraphysiological level (10 mM glutamine)

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control (0% acetic acid)
   - Treatment (0.5% acetic acid)

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Leucine supplementation at 10 mM in the absence of acetic acid.

5. **Experimental Combinations:**
   - Each glutamine level with both acetic acid levels.
   - Include negative control with and without acetic acid.

6. **Measurement:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for growth curves.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels.
   - Assess viability and growth in response to acetic acid.

7. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Ensure that glutamine supplementation effectively modulates intracellular glutamine levels.
   - Confirm that acetic acid concentration used is relevant for inducing stress without complete inhibition of growth.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- **Control Glutamine (0 mM):** Expect reduced growth and viability under acetic acid stress, supporting the association.
- **Physiological Glutamine (1 mM):** Improved resistance to acetic acid compared to control, indicating a dose-dependent relationship.
- **Supraphysiological Glutamine (10 mM):** Further enhanced resistance, demonstrating that glutamine supplementation can mitigate acetic acid stress.
- **Negative Control (Leucine):** No significant change in resistance to acetic acid, confirming that the observed effects are specific to glutamine.
  
The experiment will determine if intracellular glutamine levels directly correlate with resistance to acetic acid, supporting or refuting the association suggested in the statement.
