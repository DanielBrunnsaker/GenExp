### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower levels of intracellular glutamine and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into metabolic pathways that influence stress resistance in yeast. Glutamine is a key amino acid involved in nitrogen metabolism and cellular stress responses, which could affect the cell's ability to resist acetic acid-induced stress.

### Hypothesis
Lower intracellular glutamine levels lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae by affecting nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways, thereby increasing susceptibility to acetic acid stress.

### Experimental Plan
1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Glutamine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No glutamine supplementation.
   - Low: 0.5 mM glutamine (to simulate lower intracellular levels).
   - High: 10 mM glutamine (to simulate higher intracellular levels, including supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 20 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use leucine at 10 mM as a negative control to ensure that any observed effects are specific to glutamine modulation.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - 96-well plate format with each well representing a unique combination of glutamine and acetic acid levels.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - OD600 measurements every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours for metabolomics analysis.

6. **Condition Combinations:**
   - No glutamine, no acetic acid.
   - Low glutamine, no acetic acid.
   - High glutamine, no acetic acid.
   - No glutamine, 20 mM acetic acid.
   - Low glutamine, 20 mM acetic acid.
   - High glutamine, 20 mM acetic acid.
   - Negative control (10 mM leucine, no acetic acid).
   - Negative control (10 mM leucine, 20 mM acetic acid).

7. **Measurements:**
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels at the end of the experiment.
   - Analyze growth curves to assess resistance to acetic acid.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Control Conditions:** Cells with no glutamine supplementation and no acetic acid should show normal growth.
- **Glutamine Supplementation Without Acetic Acid:** Higher glutamine supplementation may enhance growth due to improved nitrogen metabolism.
- **Acetic Acid Treatment:** 
  - Cells with low glutamine supplementation are expected to show decreased resistance to acetic acid, indicated by reduced growth and viability.
  - Cells with high glutamine supplementation may exhibit increased resistance to acetic acid, reflected in better growth compared to low glutamine conditions.
- **Negative Control:** Leucine supplementation should not significantly affect resistance to acetic acid, confirming that the observed effects are specific to glutamine modulation.

This experimental design aims to test the association between intracellular glutamine levels and resistance to acetic acid, providing insights into the metabolic pathways involved in stress resistance.
