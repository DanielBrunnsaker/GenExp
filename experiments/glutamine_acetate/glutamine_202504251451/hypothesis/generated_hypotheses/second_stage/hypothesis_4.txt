**Motivation**

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship can provide insights into the metabolic pathways that influence stress resistance in yeast. Glutamine is a key amino acid involved in nitrogen metabolism and may play a role in stress response mechanisms.

**Hypothesis**

Lower intracellular levels of glutamine reduce the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to acetic acid by affecting pathways involved in stress response, such as the synthesis of protective metabolites or the regulation of stress-responsive genes.

**Experimental Plan**

1. **Baseline Conditions:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids for all experiments.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control (no glutamine supplementation)
   - Physiological level of glutamine supplementation: 2 mM
   - Supraphysiological level of glutamine supplementation: 10 mM

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control (no acetic acid)
   - 0.5% (v/v) acetic acid

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with threonine supplementation at 10 mM (equimolar to the highest glutamine concentration) under both acetic acid treatment levels to ensure specificity of glutamine's effect.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Combine each glutamine supplementation level with each acetic acid treatment level.
   - Each condition will be tested in triplicate.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels and other relevant metabolites.

7. **Assumption Verification:**
   - Include an experiment to verify that glutamine supplementation indeed modulates intracellular glutamine levels. Compare intracellular glutamine levels across the three supplementation conditions without acetic acid treatment.

**Expected Outcomes**

- If the hypothesis is correct, cells with lower intracellular glutamine (control or low supplementation) are expected to show decreased growth and viability in the presence of acetic acid compared to those with higher glutamine levels.
- Supraphysiological glutamine levels should enhance resistance to acetic acid, evidenced by better growth and viability.
- The negative control with threonine should not exhibit enhanced resistance, confirming the specific role of glutamine in modulating acetic acid resistance.
- Mass spectrometry will verify that glutamine supplementation effectively modulates intracellular levels and will help identify any additional metabolites involved in the response to acetic acid stress.
