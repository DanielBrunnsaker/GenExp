### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship can provide insights into the metabolic pathways and stress responses of yeast. Glutamine is a key amino acid involved in nitrogen metabolism and cellular protection mechanisms, and acetic acid is a common stressor that can affect yeast viability.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular levels of glutamine reduce the cellular resistance to acetic acid by impairing metabolic pathways that are critical for stress response, possibly through compromised nitrogen metabolism and impaired synthesis of protective compounds.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Objective**: Test the association between intracellular glutamine levels and resistance to acetic acid.

2. **Strain**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.

3. **Media**: Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline.

4. **Supplementation Levels**:
   - **Glutamine**: 
     - Control: 0 mM (no supplementation)
     - Low: 0.5 mM
     - High: 5 mM (supraphysiological level)

5. **Treatment Levels**:
   - **Acetic Acid**:
     - Control: 0 mM
     - Treatment: 50 mM (a concentration known to induce stress in yeast)

6. **Negative Control**:
   - Use leucine at 5 mM as a negative control to ensure that the observed effects are specific to glutamine modulation.

7. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Total combinations: 3 glutamine levels × 2 acetic acid levels = 6 conditions, plus treated and untreated negative controls.
   - Each condition will be set up in a separate well of a 96-well plate.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

8. **Verification**:
   - Measure intracellular glutamine levels at the end of the experiment using mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to confirm modulation.

9. **Experiment Termination**: After 20 hours.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions** (0 mM glutamine, 0 mM acetic acid): Normal growth, serving as a baseline.
- **Glutamine Supplementation**:
  - **Low Glutamine (0.5 mM)**: Expect decreased growth in the presence of acetic acid compared to control, indicating reduced resistance.
  - **High Glutamine (5 mM)**: Expect increased resistance to acetic acid, potentially showing improved growth compared to low glutamine levels.
- **Negative Control** (Leucine): No significant change in resistance to acetic acid, confirming that effects are specific to glutamine.

This experiment will elucidate the role of glutamine in acetic acid resistance, supporting or refuting the proposed association.
