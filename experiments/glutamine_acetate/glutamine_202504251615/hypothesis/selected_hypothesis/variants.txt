### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Glutamine is a critical amino acid involved in various cellular processes, including nitrogen metabolism and stress response. Understanding this association could provide insights into the role of glutamine in stress resistance mechanisms.

### Hypothesis

Cells with reduced intracellular glutamine levels exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid due to impaired nitrogen metabolism, which compromises the cellular stress response pathways.

### Experimental Plan

**Objective:** To determine if varying intracellular glutamine levels affect acetic acid resistance in S. cerevisiae.

**Media and Conditions:**
- **Base Medium:** Minimal media without amino acids.
- **Temperature:** 30°C
- **Duration:** 20 hours
- **Shaking:** Standard conditions

**Supplementation and Treatment Levels:**
1. **Glutamine Supplementation:**
   - Control: 0 mM Glutamine
   - Low: 1 mM Glutamine
   - High: 10 mM Glutamine (supraphysiological concentration)

2. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: 0 mM Acetic Acid
   - Treatment: 50 mM Acetic Acid (a concentration known to stress yeast cells)

**Negative Control:**
- **Amino Acid Supplementation:** Leucine at 10 mM (equimolar to the highest glutamine concentration) to serve as a negative control for amino acid supplementation effects.

**Experimental Conditions:**
- Total of 8 conditions (3 glutamine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + negative control):
  1. 0 mM Glutamine + 0 mM Acetic Acid
  2. 1 mM Glutamine + 0 mM Acetic Acid
  3. 10 mM Glutamine + 0 mM Acetic Acid
  4. 0 mM Glutamine + 50 mM Acetic Acid
  5. 1 mM Glutamine + 50 mM Acetic Acid
  6. 10 mM Glutamine + 50 mM Acetic Acid
  7. Negative Control: 10 mM Leucine + 0 mM Acetic Acid
  8. Negative Control: 10 mM Leucine + 50 mM Acetic Acid

**Measurements:**
- **Growth Curves:** OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
- **Metabolomics:** Mass spectrometry at experiment termination to measure intracellular glutamine levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions (0 mM Acetic Acid):** Increasing glutamine should not significantly affect growth, but intracellular glutamine levels should increase with supplementation.
  
- **Acetic Acid Treatment (50 mM):** 
  - **Low Glutamine (0 mM):** Expect decreased growth and viability, supporting the hypothesis that low glutamine levels impair stress resistance.
  - **Moderate (1 mM) and High (10 mM) Glutamine:** Improved growth and viability compared to low glutamine, suggesting enhanced stress resistance with higher glutamine levels.

- **Negative Control:** Leucine supplementation should not affect acetic acid resistance, confirming that observed effects are specific to glutamine.

This experimental setup will help determine if intracellular glutamine levels are indeed associated with acetic acid resistance in yeast, providing insights into the underlying mechanisms.
