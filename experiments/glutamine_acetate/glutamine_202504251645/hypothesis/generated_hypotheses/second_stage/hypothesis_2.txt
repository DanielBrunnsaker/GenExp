**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between low intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic pathways that influence chemical resistance and potentially identify targets for engineering yeast strains with enhanced resistance.

**Hypothesis:**
Lower intracellular glutamine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to glutamine's role in nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways. This decrease in resistance could manifest as reduced growth or viability when exposed to acetic acid.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No glutamine supplementation.
   - Low glutamine: Supplement with 0.5 mM glutamine.
   - High glutamine: Supplement with 10 mM glutamine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use threonine as a negative control at 10 mM (equimolar to the high glutamine level).

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Incubate all cultures at 30°C with shaking.
   - Start all supplementations and treatments at t=0h.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels.

6. **Experimental Combinations:**
   - No glutamine, no acetic acid (baseline control).
   - Low glutamine, no acetic acid.
   - High glutamine, no acetic acid.
   - No glutamine, 50 mM acetic acid.
   - Low glutamine, 50 mM acetic acid.
   - High glutamine, 50 mM acetic acid.
   - Threonine control, no acetic acid.
   - Threonine control, 50 mM acetic acid.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- In the absence of acetic acid, glutamine supplementation should not significantly affect growth, as it is a non-limiting condition.
- In the presence of acetic acid, cultures with no or low glutamine supplementation are expected to show decreased growth or viability, supporting the hypothesis that low glutamine levels are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid.
- High glutamine supplementation may mitigate the adverse effects of acetic acid, suggesting a protective role of glutamine.
- The threonine control should not exhibit significant changes in resistance, confirming that the observed effects are specific to glutamine.
