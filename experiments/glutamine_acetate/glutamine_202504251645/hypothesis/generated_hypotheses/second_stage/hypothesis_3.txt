**Motivation:**

The statement suggests that lower intracellular levels of glutamine are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Glutamine is a key amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways, including nitrogen metabolism and stress response. By modulating intracellular glutamine levels, we can examine whether there is a correlation with acetic acid resistance, potentially providing insights into the metabolic factors influencing stress resistance.

**Hypothesis:**

Cells with reduced intracellular glutamine levels will exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to impaired nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
   - Prepare three different concentrations of glutamine supplementation: 
     - Control (0 mM)
     - Physiological concentration (2 mM)
     - Supraphysiological concentration (10 mM)

2. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Acetic acid will be added to the media at a concentration of 50 mM, which is known to challenge yeast cells without causing immediate lethality.

3. **Negative Control:**
   - Use leucine as a negative control, supplementing at 10 mM, to ensure that the observed effects are specific to glutamine modulation.

4. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - 3 glutamine levels x 2 acetic acid conditions (treated and untreated) = 6 conditions
   - Include the leucine control with and without acetic acid (2 additional conditions)
   - Total of 8 experimental conditions

5. **Procedure:**
   - Inoculate each well with the reference prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Add the respective glutamine or leucine supplementation and acetic acid at t=0h.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels at the end of the experiment.

6. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Confirm that glutamine levels are effectively modulated by the supplementation through metabolomics.
   - Ensure that acetic acid treatment is consistent across conditions.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- If the hypothesis is correct, cells with lower intracellular glutamine levels (0 mM supplementation) will show reduced growth and viability in the presence of acetic acid compared to those with higher glutamine levels.
- Cells supplemented with physiological and supraphysiological glutamine concentrations should exhibit better resistance to acetic acid, reflected in higher OD600 readings and viability.
- The leucine control should not show significant differences in acetic acid resistance, confirming the specificity of the glutamine effect.
- This experiment will help determine if the association between glutamine levels and acetic acid resistance is valid and provide insights into the underlying metabolic mechanisms.
