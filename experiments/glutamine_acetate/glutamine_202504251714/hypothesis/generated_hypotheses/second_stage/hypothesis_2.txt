**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into metabolic pathways and stress responses in yeast, which are relevant for industrial fermentation processes where acetic acid is a common byproduct.

**Hypothesis:**
Lower intracellular glutamine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae result in decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to glutamine's role in nitrogen metabolism and stress response pathways. This could be mediated through the impact on pathways such as the TOR signaling pathway, which is sensitive to nutrient availability.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids, unless specified otherwise.

2. **Supplementations:**
   - Modulate intracellular glutamine levels by supplementing media with different concentrations of glutamine:
     - Control: 0 mM glutamine
     - Low: 0.1 mM glutamine (to simulate lower physiological levels)
     - High: 10 mM glutamine (supraphysiological level)

3. **Treatment:**
   - Treat cells with acetic acid to test resistance:
     - Control: 0 mM acetic acid
     - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid (a concentration known to affect yeast growth)

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control using threonine at 10 mM, equimolar to the highest glutamine concentration, as it is not directly involved in the pathways related to acetic acid resistance.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Set up a 96-well plate with all combinations of glutamine supplementation and acetic acid treatment.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability over 20 hours.
   - At experiment termination, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamine levels.

6. **Experimental Combinations:**
   - 3 glutamine levels x 2 acetic acid treatments = 6 conditions
   - Add treated and untreated negative control with threonine: 2 conditions
   - Total: 8 experimental conditions

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If lower glutamine levels result in decreased resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see reduced growth (lower OD600) in the low glutamine + acetic acid condition compared to the control and high glutamine conditions.
- The high glutamine condition may show improved growth in the presence of acetic acid, indicating a protective effect.
- The threonine negative control should not show significant changes in resistance to acetic acid, confirming the specificity of the glutamine effect.
- Mass spectrometry should confirm the modulation of intracellular glutamine levels corresponding to supplementation levels.
