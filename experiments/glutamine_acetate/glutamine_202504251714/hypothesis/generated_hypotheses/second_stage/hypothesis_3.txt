### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between low intracellular glutamine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Glutamine is a key nitrogen source and a precursor for many biosynthetic pathways, potentially influencing stress responses. Acetic acid is a known stressor that affects yeast growth and viability. This experiment aims to explore whether modulating intracellular glutamine levels affects yeast resistance to acetic acid.

### Hypothesis

Reducing intracellular glutamine levels in S. cerevisiae will decrease its resistance to acetic acid, leading to reduced growth and viability compared to cells with normal or elevated glutamine levels.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media (MM) without amino acids as the baseline.

2. **Glutamine Supplementation Levels**
   - Control: No glutamine supplementation.
   - Low Glutamine: 0.5 mM glutamine.
   - High Glutamine: 10 mM glutamine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Acetic Acid Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control**
   - Use threonine as a negative control at 10 mM, equimolar to the highest glutamine supplementation.

5. **Experimental Conditions**
   - Total conditions: 3 (glutamine levels) x 2 (acetic acid levels) + 2 (threonine controls) = 8 conditions.
   - Incubate all conditions at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours for metabolomic analysis.

6. **Metabolomic Analysis**
   - Use mass spectrometry to measure intracellular glutamine levels at experiment termination.
   - Verify glutamine modulation and assess any correlation with acetic acid resistance.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Low Glutamine, Acetic Acid Treatment**: Expect decreased growth and viability compared to other conditions, supporting the hypothesis that low glutamine levels reduce acetic acid resistance.
- **High Glutamine, Acetic Acid Treatment**: Potentially improved resistance to acetic acid, with higher growth rates compared to the low glutamine condition.
- **Threonine Controls**: No significant change in growth or acetic acid resistance, confirming that observed effects are specific to glutamine modulation.
- **Metabolomics**: Successful modulation of intracellular glutamine levels, confirming the experimental setup's effectiveness.

This experiment will help determine if intracellular glutamine levels directly influence yeast resistance to acetic acid, providing insights into the metabolic regulation of stress responses in S. cerevisiae.
