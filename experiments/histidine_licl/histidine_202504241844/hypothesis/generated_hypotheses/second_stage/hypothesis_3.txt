### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Zinc dichloride is known to be a chemical stressor, and histidine may play a role in metal ion homeostasis or stress response. To explore this association, I will design an experiment to modulate intracellular histidine levels and assess the impact on resistance to zinc dichloride.

### Hypothesis
Reducing intracellular histidine levels will lead to decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, as histidine may be involved in buffering cells against metal ion stress or maintaining cellular integrity under chemical stress.

### Experimental Plan
1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Histidine Modulation:**
   - Supplement media with histidine at three levels:
     - Control: 0 mM (no supplementation)
     - Low: 0.5 mM (to reduce intracellular histidine)
     - High: 5 mM (supraphysiological concentration to increase intracellular histidine)

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment:**
   - Treat cells with zinc dichloride at a concentration known to induce stress in yeast: 0 mM (control) and 10 mM (stressor condition).

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control using leucine at 5 mM (equimolar to the highest histidine supplementation) to confirm that observed effects are specific to histidine modulation and not due to general amino acid supplementation.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total conditions: 3 histidine levels x 2 zinc dichloride levels + 1 leucine control x 2 zinc dichloride levels = 8 conditions.
   - Conduct all treatments at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours for metabolomics analysis.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics at experiment termination to measure intracellular histidine levels.
   - Assess viability and growth curves to determine resistance to zinc dichloride.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Histidine Modulation:**
  - Low histidine supplementation is expected to decrease intracellular histidine levels, potentially reducing resistance to zinc dichloride.
  - High histidine supplementation may increase intracellular histidine levels, potentially enhancing resistance to zinc dichloride.

- **Zinc Dichloride Effects:**
  - In the absence of histidine supplementation, zinc dichloride is expected to reduce growth and viability, serving as a baseline for chemical resistance.

- **Negative Control:**
  - Leucine supplementation should not affect resistance to zinc dichloride, confirming that changes in resistance are specific to histidine modulation.

This experiment will help clarify the role of histidine in chemical stress resistance and validate the association between intracellular histidine levels and response to zinc dichloride.
