### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an amino acid that can play various roles in cellular metabolism, including metal ion chelation. Thus, understanding whether histidine levels influence resistance to zinc dichloride could provide insights into mechanisms of metal ion detoxification or tolerance.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular levels of histidine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae lead to decreased resistance to zinc dichloride by potentially affecting metal ion chelation or cellular stress response pathways.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Histidine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low Histidine: 50 µM histidine.
   - High Histidine: 500 µM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Zinc Dichloride: 100 µM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with 500 µM of serine, an amino acid not directly involved in metal ion chelation or stress response, ensuring it is relevant as a negative control for this type of experiment.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Each combination of histidine supplementation and zinc dichloride treatment will be set up in separate wells of a 96-well plate.
   - The combinations are:
     - No histidine + No zinc dichloride
     - No histidine + Zinc dichloride
     - Low histidine + No zinc dichloride
     - Low histidine + Zinc dichloride
     - High histidine + No zinc dichloride
     - High histidine + Zinc dichloride
     - Serine + No zinc dichloride
     - Serine + Zinc dichloride

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At the end of the 20-hour experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels.

7. **Assumptions and Controls:**
   - Ensure that histidine supplementation affects intracellular levels by comparing the mass spectrometry results across conditions.
   - Verify that zinc dichloride impacts growth by comparing growth curves with and without zinc dichloride.

### Expected Outcomes

- If lower intracellular histidine is indeed associated with decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, then wells with no or low histidine supplementation and zinc dichloride treatment should show reduced growth compared to wells with high histidine supplementation.
- Wells with serine supplementation should not exhibit significant changes in resistance to zinc dichloride, serving as a baseline to confirm that observed effects are specific to histidine.
- The mass spectrometry results should confirm that histidine supplementation correlates with intracellular histidine levels, validating the modulation approach.
- This experiment will help elucidate whether histidine plays a role in zinc dichloride resistance, potentially through mechanisms like metal ion chelation or stress response modulation.
