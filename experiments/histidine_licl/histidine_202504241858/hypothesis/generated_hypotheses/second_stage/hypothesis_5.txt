### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is known to play a role in metal ion homeostasis and stress response, which could influence the resistance of yeast cells to certain chemicals, including zinc dichloride. This experiment aims to test whether modulating intracellular histidine levels affects the resistance of yeast cells to zinc dichloride.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular histidine levels will lead to decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae due to impaired metal ion homeostasis and stress response mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids unless specified otherwise.

2. **Histidine Supplementation:**
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low Level: 1 mM histidine.
   - High Level: 10 mM histidine.

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment:**
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Treatment: 1 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use valine at 10 mM as a negative control to ensure that any observed effects are specific to histidine modulation.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total conditions: 3 histidine levels x 2 zinc dichloride levels + 2 negative control conditions = 8 conditions.
   - Each condition will be set up in triplicate in a 96-well plate format.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Add all supplementations and treatments at the start of the experiment (t=0h).
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and determine viability.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels.

6. **Assumptions Verification:**
   - Ensure that the histidine supplementation effectively modulates intracellular histidine levels by comparing metabolomics data across different supplementation levels.
   - Verify that zinc dichloride treatment is effective by comparing growth curves of treated vs. untreated cells.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control (No Histidine, No Zinc Dichloride):** Normal growth and baseline resistance to zinc dichloride.
- **Low Histidine, No Zinc Dichloride:** Reduced intracellular histidine levels, normal growth.
- **High Histidine, No Zinc Dichloride:** Increased intracellular histidine levels, normal growth.
- **Control (No Histidine, Zinc Dichloride):** Reduced growth due to zinc dichloride stress.
- **Low Histidine, Zinc Dichloride:** Further reduced growth compared to control zinc dichloride treatment, supporting the hypothesis of decreased resistance.
- **High Histidine, Zinc Dichloride:** Potentially improved growth compared to control zinc dichloride treatment, indicating enhanced resistance.
- **Negative Control (Valine):** No significant change in growth or resistance, confirming that observed effects are specific to histidine modulation.

The experiment will provide insights into the role of intracellular histidine in mediating resistance to zinc dichloride, potentially supporting the association stated.
