####### OPTION 1 #######

### Motivation

The goal of this experiment is to investigate whether lower levels of intracellular histidine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with decreased resistance to zinc dichloride. Zinc is an essential trace element but can be toxic at high concentrations. Histidine is an amino acid that can bind metal ions, potentially influencing the cell's response to metal stress.

### Hypothesis

Cells with reduced intracellular histidine levels will exhibit decreased resistance to zinc dichloride due to histidine's role in metal ion binding and detoxification. This may lead to increased sensitivity to zinc-induced stress.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: 
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Histidine Supplementation Levels**:
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low Histidine: 0.1 mM histidine.
   - High Histidine: 1 mM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment Levels**:
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Zinc Treatment: 0.5 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use serine as a negative control at 1 mM (equimolar to the highest histidine level).

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Each well in the 96-well plate will contain one of the following combinations:
     - No histidine, no zinc dichloride
     - No histidine, 0.5 mM zinc dichloride
     - 0.1 mM histidine, no zinc dichloride
     - 0.1 mM histidine, 0.5 mM zinc dichloride
     - 1 mM histidine, no zinc dichloride
     - 1 mM histidine, 0.5 mM zinc dichloride
     - Serine control, no zinc dichloride
     - Serine control, 0.5 mM zinc dichloride

6. **Incubation**:
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

7. **Termination and Measurement**:
   - Terminate experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions**: Cells with no histidine supplementation and no zinc dichloride should show normal growth, serving as a baseline.
  
- **Histidine Supplementation**:
  - Low histidine may lead to slightly reduced growth in the presence of zinc dichloride compared to high histidine supplementation, supporting the hypothesis.
  - High histidine supplementation might mitigate zinc toxicity, showing better growth in zinc dichloride presence compared to low histidine levels.

- **Negative Control**: Serine should not affect zinc resistance, confirming that the observed effects are specific to histidine.

If cells with lower histidine levels show decreased resistance to zinc, it will support the hypothesis that intracellular histidine levels are associated with zinc resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
