### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower levels of intracellular histidine and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an essential amino acid that plays a role in various cellular processes, including metal ion chelation and buffering capacity. It is plausible that histidine levels could influence cellular resistance to metal ions like zinc.

### Hypothesis
Cells with lower intracellular histidine levels exhibit decreased resistance to zinc dichloride due to impaired metal ion chelation, which leads to increased sensitivity to zinc toxicity.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Objective**: To test whether varying intracellular histidine levels affect resistance to zinc dichloride in S. cerevisiae.

2. **Strain**: Use a wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.

3. **Media and Conditions**:
   - Base media: Minimal media without amino acids.
   - Temperature: 30°C
   - Shaking: 200 rpm

4. **Supplementation Levels**:
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low histidine: 0.1 mM histidine.
   - High histidine: 10 mM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

5. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment**:
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Treatment: 1 mM zinc dichloride.

6. **Negative Control**:
   - Use valine at 10 mM as a negative control to ensure that the observed effects are specific to histidine modulation and not due to general amino acid supplementation.

7. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Combine each histidine supplementation level with the zinc dichloride treatment.
   - Total combinations: 3 (histidine levels) x 2 (zinc dichloride levels) + 2 (valine controls) = 8 conditions.

8. **Measurements**:
   - OD600 measurements every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine and zinc levels.

9. **Verification**:
   - Ensure histidine uptake by measuring intracellular histidine levels in the absence of zinc dichloride.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control (No histidine, no zinc dichloride)**: Normal growth, baseline intracellular histidine levels.
- **Low histidine, no zinc dichloride**: Reduced intracellular histidine levels, normal growth.
- **High histidine, no zinc dichloride**: Elevated intracellular histidine levels, normal growth.
- **Control (No histidine, with zinc dichloride)**: Decreased growth, indicating sensitivity to zinc.
- **Low histidine, with zinc dichloride**: Further reduced growth compared to control, supporting the hypothesis that lower histidine levels decrease resistance to zinc.
- **High histidine, with zinc dichloride**: Possible improved growth compared to control, suggesting that higher histidine levels may enhance resistance to zinc.
- **Valine controls**: Should show no significant change in growth or resistance to zinc dichloride, confirming that the effects are specific to histidine modulation.

The experiment will provide insights into the role of histidine in modulating resistance to zinc dichloride, supporting or refuting the association stated.
