**Motivation:**
The aim of this experiment is to investigate whether lower intracellular levels of histidine in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with decreased resistance to zinc dichloride. Understanding this association could provide insights into the role of histidine in cellular resistance mechanisms against chemical stressors.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with reduced intracellular histidine levels exhibit decreased resistance to zinc dichloride due to impaired buffering capacity or metal ion chelation, which normally helps mitigate the toxic effects of heavy metals like zinc.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Baseline media: Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Histidine supplementation:
     - Control: No histidine added.
     - Low level: 10 µM histidine.
     - High level: 100 µM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Treatment Levels:**
   - Zinc dichloride exposure:
     - Control: No zinc dichloride.
     - Treated: 1 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use serine as a negative control to ensure specificity of the histidine effect, provided at 100 µM.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Each well in the 96-well plate will contain one of the following combinations:
     - Control (no histidine, no zinc dichloride)
     - Low histidine, no zinc dichloride
     - High histidine, no zinc dichloride
     - Control (no histidine, with zinc dichloride)
     - Low histidine, with zinc dichloride
     - High histidine, with zinc dichloride
     - Serine control, no zinc dichloride
     - Serine control, with zinc dichloride

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels.

7. **Assumptions:**
   - Zinc dichloride at 1 mM is sufficient to induce a measurable stress response in yeast cells.
   - Histidine levels can be modulated effectively through supplementation.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- **If the hypothesis is correct:** Cells with lower intracellular histidine (no supplementation or low level) will show decreased growth and viability in the presence of zinc dichloride compared to cells with higher histidine levels. This would indicate an association between histidine levels and resistance to zinc dichloride.
- **If the hypothesis is incorrect:** There will be no significant difference in growth or viability across different histidine supplementation levels when exposed to zinc dichloride, suggesting that histidine does not play a significant role in resistance to this compound.
- **Independent effects:** Histidine supplementation alone may enhance growth due to its role in protein synthesis, independent of zinc dichloride treatment. The serine control should not show a significant change in resistance to zinc dichloride, confirming the specificity of the histidine effect.
