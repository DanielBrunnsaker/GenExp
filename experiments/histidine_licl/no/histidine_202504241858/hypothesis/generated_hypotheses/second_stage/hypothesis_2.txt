### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an amino acid that plays roles in protein synthesis, enzyme function, and metal ion binding. Understanding whether different intracellular levels of histidine affect the yeast's resistance to zinc dichloride can provide insights into the potential protective roles of histidine against chemical stress.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular levels of histidine in S. cerevisiae are associated with decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, possibly due to histidine's role in metal ion binding and detoxification processes. By modulating histidine levels, we can observe changes in yeast's growth in the presence of zinc dichloride, providing insight into this association.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain. Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids, except for specific supplementations.

2. **Supplementation Levels**:
   - **Histidine Supplementation**: 
     - Control: No histidine.
     - Low Histidine: 0.1 mM.
     - High Histidine: 5 mM (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment**:
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Treatment: 1 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control**: Use valine at 5 mM as a negative control. Valine is not expected to interact with zinc dichloride resistance mechanisms and serves as a baseline for growth under supplemented amino acid conditions.

5. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Grow cultures in 96-well plates with the following combinations:
     - Histidine levels (0 mM, 0.1 mM, 5 mM) x Zinc dichloride levels (0 mM, 1 mM).
     - Include valine control with and without zinc dichloride.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for growth curves.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine concentrations.

6. **Verification of Assumptions**:
   - Ensure that histidine supplementation effectively modulates intracellular levels by comparing metabolomics data across different supplementation levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions**: Cells with no histidine supplementation and no zinc dichloride should exhibit normal growth.
- **Histidine Supplementation**: 
  - Low histidine may show reduced growth in zinc dichloride compared to high histidine, supporting the association.
  - High histidine supplementation is expected to enhance resistance to zinc dichloride, indicated by better growth compared to low histidine.
- **Valine Control**: Growth should be consistent across zinc dichloride treatments, indicating the specificity of histidine's effect.

By comparing growth across these conditions, we can determine if lower intracellular histidine correlates with decreased zinc dichloride resistance, thereby supporting or refuting the statement.
