**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an essential amino acid known to play a role in metal ion binding and homeostasis. This experiment aims to test if modulating intracellular histidine levels affects the yeast's resistance to zinc dichloride.

**Hypothesis:**
Reducing intracellular histidine levels in S. cerevisiae will decrease the yeast's resistance to zinc dichloride due to histidine's role in metal ion chelation and cellular stress responses.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Histidine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low Histidine: 0.1 mM histidine.
   - High Histidine (Supraphysiological): 2.0 mM histidine.

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Zinc Dichloride: 0.5 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use serine as a negative control at 2.0 mM, equimolar to the highest histidine concentration.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Divide the 96-well plate to accommodate all combinations of histidine supplementation and zinc dichloride treatment, including negative controls.
   - Each condition will be prepared in triplicate to ensure statistical reliability.

6. **Growth and Viability Measurement:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels.

7. **Justification:**
   - Histidine is supplemented to modulate its intracellular levels and observe the effect on zinc dichloride resistance.
   - Zinc dichloride is used at a concentration known to induce stress in yeast cells, allowing for observation of resistance changes.
   - Serine is chosen as a negative control due to its non-involvement in metal ion binding.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- **Control (No zinc dichloride):** Cells with higher histidine supplementation should grow normally, indicating that histidine alone does not negatively impact growth.
- **Zinc Dichloride Treatment:**
  - Cells with no or low histidine supplementation are expected to show decreased growth and viability, supporting the hypothesis that lower histidine levels reduce resistance to zinc dichloride.
  - Cells with high histidine supplementation may exhibit better growth and viability, suggesting that increased histidine levels confer protection against zinc-induced stress.
- **Negative Control (Serine):** Should show no significant difference in growth or resistance to zinc dichloride across all conditions, confirming that the observed effects are specific to histidine modulation.
