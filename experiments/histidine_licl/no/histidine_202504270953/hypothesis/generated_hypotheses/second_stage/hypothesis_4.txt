### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an amino acid that plays a role in various cellular processes, including metal ion homeostasis. This experiment aims to investigate whether modulating intracellular histidine levels affects the yeast's resistance to zinc dichloride.

### Hypothesis

If intracellular histidine levels are reduced, Saccharomyces cerevisiae will exhibit decreased resistance to zinc dichloride due to histidine's role in metal ion binding and detoxification processes.

### Experimental Plan

**1. Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Baseline media: Minimal media without amino acids.

**2. Supplementation Levels:**
   - Histidine supplementation to modulate intracellular levels:
     - Control: No histidine supplementation.
     - Low: 0.1 mM histidine.
     - High: 5 mM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

**3. Zinc Dichloride Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Treatment: 1 mM zinc dichloride.

**4. Negative Control:**
   - Use valine as the negative control at 5 mM (equimolar to the highest histidine concentration) to ensure specificity of histidine's effect.

**5. Experimental Conditions:**
   - Set up a 96-well plate with the following combinations:
     - Histidine levels: Control, 0.1 mM, 5 mM.
     - Zinc dichloride levels: Control, 1 mM.
     - Include valine negative control with and without zinc dichloride.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

**6. Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth curves and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine and zinc concentrations.

**7. Verification:**
   - Ensure that histidine supplementation effectively modulates intracellular histidine levels using metabolomics data.

**Total Conditions:**
   - (3 histidine levels) x (2 zinc dichloride levels) + 2 (valine controls) = 8 conditions.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Histidine (5 mM):** Cells are expected to maintain or increase resistance to zinc dichloride, reflected by normal or enhanced growth curves and viability, due to adequate histidine levels aiding in detoxification.
- **Decreased Histidine (0.1 mM):** Cells may show decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, as indicated by reduced growth or viability, supporting the hypothesis that lower histidine impairs resistance mechanisms.
- **Valine Control:** No significant change in resistance to zinc dichloride should be observed, confirming that the observed effects are specific to histidine modulation.
- **Mass Spectrometry Results:** Should confirm that histidine levels are effectively modulated by supplementation, and any changes in zinc accumulation correlate with the resistance phenotype.

This experimental setup will help elucidate the role of histidine in zinc dichloride resistance in S. cerevisiae.
