### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an essential amino acid that plays roles in various metabolic pathways, including protein synthesis and metal ion binding. Zinc is a vital trace element but can be toxic at high concentrations. Understanding how intracellular histidine levels influence zinc resistance could provide insights into metal ion homeostasis and stress responses in yeast.

### Hypothesis

Decreasing intracellular histidine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will result in decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, potentially due to histidine's role in metal ion chelation and buffering, which may protect against zinc toxicity.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the base medium.

2. **Histidine Supplementation:**
   - Level 1: 0 mM histidine (control, no supplementation)
   - Level 2: 0.1 mM histidine (physiological concentration)
   - Level 3: 1 mM histidine (supraphysiological concentration)

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment:**
   - Level 1: 0 mM zinc dichloride (control, no treatment)
   - Level 2: 0.5 mM zinc dichloride (sub-lethal concentration)
   - Level 3: 2 mM zinc dichloride (lethal concentration)

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use 0.1 mM lysine as a negative control to ensure that observed effects are specific to histidine manipulation.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Combine each histidine supplementation level with each zinc dichloride treatment level, resulting in 9 conditions (3 histidine levels x 3 zinc levels).
   - Include treated and untreated negative control (lysine) for a total of 11 conditions.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and viability.
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Histidine Supplementation Effects:**
  - Increasing histidine supplementation should increase intracellular histidine levels.
  - Higher histidine levels may enhance resistance to zinc dichloride by buffering zinc toxicity.

- **Zinc Dichloride Treatment Effects:**
  - Increasing zinc dichloride concentrations should decrease cell viability, particularly at higher concentrations.

- **Interaction Effects:**
  - Cells with lower intracellular histidine (0 mM supplementation) are expected to show decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, especially at higher zinc concentrations.
  - If histidine plays a role in zinc resistance, cells with higher histidine levels (1 mM supplementation) should exhibit increased resistance to zinc dichloride toxicity compared to the control.

- **Negative Control:**
  - Lysine supplementation should not affect resistance to zinc dichloride, confirming the specificity of histidine's role.

This experimental setup will allow us to evaluate the association between intracellular histidine levels and zinc dichloride resistance, while controlling for confounding factors.
