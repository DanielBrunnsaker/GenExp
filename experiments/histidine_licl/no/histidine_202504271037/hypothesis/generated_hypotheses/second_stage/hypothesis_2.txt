### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an essential amino acid that plays a role in various cellular processes, including protein synthesis and enzyme activity. Zinc dichloride is a chemical compound that can be toxic to cells at certain concentrations. Understanding the relationship between histidine levels and zinc dichloride resistance could provide insights into cellular stress responses and amino acid metabolism.

### Hypothesis

Cells with reduced intracellular histidine levels exhibit decreased resistance to zinc dichloride due to impaired histidine-dependent cellular processes, such as protein synthesis or enzyme function, that are critical for detoxifying or mitigating the effects of zinc dichloride.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Setup:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.

2. **Histidine Supplementation:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No histidine supplementation.
   - Level 2: 50 µM histidine (physiological concentration).
   - Level 3: 500 µM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No zinc dichloride treatment.
   - Level 2: 0.5 mM zinc dichloride.
   - Level 3: 1.0 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with 500 µM of an irrelevant amino acid, such as valine, in both treated and untreated conditions to ensure no confounding effects related to amino acid supplementation.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Prepare a 96-well plate with each well containing one combination of histidine level and zinc dichloride treatment.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking to maintain aeration.
   - Monitor growth by measuring OD600 every 20 minutes.

6. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of the 20-hour incubation, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels and assess any changes in metabolite profiles.

7. **Verification:**
   - Verify that histidine levels are modulated as intended by comparing intracellular histidine concentrations across different supplementation levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Histidine Supplementation Effects:**
  - Increased intracellular histidine levels in supplemented conditions compared to control.
  - The supraphysiological concentration may lead to saturation effects, possibly affecting other metabolic pathways.

- **Zinc Dichloride Effects:**
  - Decreased growth and viability in higher zinc dichloride concentrations, especially in conditions with no or low histidine supplementation, indicating decreased resistance.

- **Interaction Effects:**
  - If the hypothesis is correct, cells with no or low histidine supplementation will show significantly reduced growth and viability in the presence of zinc dichloride compared to those with higher histidine levels.
  - The negative control with valine should not affect zinc dichloride resistance, confirming the specific role of histidine.

This experimental design aims to elucidate the role of histidine in conferring resistance to zinc dichloride in yeast cells.
