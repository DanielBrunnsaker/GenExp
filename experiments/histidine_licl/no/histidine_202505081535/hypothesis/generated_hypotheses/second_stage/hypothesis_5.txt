### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an amino acid that can play a role in metal ion chelation and buffering, potentially affecting cellular resistance to metal stress. By modulating intracellular histidine levels, we can test whether these levels influence the yeast's resistance to zinc dichloride.

### Hypothesis
Cells with reduced intracellular histidine levels will exhibit decreased resistance to zinc dichloride due to impaired metal ion buffering and chelation, which could lead to increased susceptibility to metal-induced stress.

### Experimental Plan
1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain. Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Histidine Modulation**: 
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low Histidine: 0.1 mM histidine.
   - High Histidine: 10 mM histidine (supraphysiological level).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment**:
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Treatment: 1 mM zinc dichloride.

4. **Negative Control**: Include a negative control with 10 mM serine (equimolar to the highest histidine supplementation) to ensure that any observed effects are specific to histidine and not general amino acid supplementation.

5. **Experimental Conditions**: 
   - 96-well plate format.
   - Each well contains one of the following combinations:
     - Histidine Control + Zinc Control
     - Histidine Control + Zinc Treatment
     - Low Histidine + Zinc Control
     - Low Histidine + Zinc Treatment
     - High Histidine + Zinc Control
     - High Histidine + Zinc Treatment
     - Serine Control + Zinc Control
     - Serine Control + Zinc Treatment
   - All treatments are added at the start of the experiment (t=0h).

6. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels and confirm modulation.
   - Assess viability via growth curves.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Histidine Control + Zinc Treatment**: Expect decreased growth compared to Histidine Control + Zinc Control, reflecting susceptibility to zinc dichloride.
- **Low Histidine + Zinc Treatment**: Expect further decreased growth compared to Histidine Control + Zinc Treatment, supporting the hypothesis that lower histidine levels increase susceptibility.
- **High Histidine + Zinc Treatment**: Anticipate similar or improved resistance compared to Histidine Control + Zinc Treatment, suggesting that higher histidine levels may buffer against zinc stress.
- **Serine Control + Zinc Treatment**: Expect no significant change in resistance compared to Histidine Control + Zinc Treatment, confirming that the observed effects are specific to histidine modulation.

This experiment will help determine if intracellular histidine levels influence the resistance of S. cerevisiae to zinc dichloride, thus supporting or refuting the association in the statement.
