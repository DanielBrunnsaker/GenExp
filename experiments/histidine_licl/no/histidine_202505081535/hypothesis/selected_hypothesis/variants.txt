####### OPTION 1 #######

### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an essential amino acid involved in various cellular processes, including protein synthesis and buffering intracellular pH. Zinc is a crucial micronutrient but can be toxic at high concentrations. Understanding how histidine levels influence zinc resistance could provide insights into amino acid metabolism and stress responses.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular levels of histidine reduce the cell's ability to manage zinc-induced stress, possibly due to impaired protein synthesis or buffering capacity, leading to decreased resistance to zinc dichloride. 

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Histidine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low: 0.1 mM histidine.
   - High: 10 mM histidine (supraphysiological level).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Treatment: 1 mM zinc dichloride (a concentration known to induce stress but not lethality).

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with 10 mM arginine, equimolar to the highest histidine supplementation, to account for any amino acid supplementation effect.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Inoculate cultures in 96-well plates with shaking at 30°C.
   - Add supplements at t=0h.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for growth curves.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours for metabolomics analysis.

6. **Experimental Combinations:**
   - 3 histidine levels x 2 zinc dichloride levels = 6 conditions.
   - 2 negative control conditions (with and without zinc dichloride) = 2 conditions.
   - Total: 8 experimental conditions.

7. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Measure intracellular histidine levels via mass spectrometry to confirm modulation.
   - Assess viability and growth rates through OD600 measurements.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Histidine Modulation:**
  - Successful modulation of intracellular histidine levels across supplementation conditions.

- **Zinc Dichloride Resistance:**
  - If the hypothesis is correct, cells with lower histidine levels (control and low supplementation) will exhibit decreased growth or viability in the presence of zinc dichloride compared to high histidine supplementation.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - Arginine supplementation should not significantly affect zinc resistance, serving as a proper negative control.

This experiment will help determine if and how intracellular histidine levels influence the resistance of S. cerevisiae to zinc dichloride, providing insights into the interplay between amino acid metabolism and metal ion stress responses.
