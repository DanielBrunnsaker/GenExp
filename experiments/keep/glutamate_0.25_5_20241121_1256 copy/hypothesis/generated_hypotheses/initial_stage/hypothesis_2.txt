The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to the chemical actinomycin D in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Cells with elevated intracellular glutamate levels exhibit decreased resistance to actinomycin D due to glutamate's potential role in affecting cellular stress response pathways. 1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in a 96-well plate format using minimal media without amino acids.

   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

3. **Chemical Treatment:**
   - Treat all groups with actinomycin D at a concentration of 0.5 μg/mL at the start of the experiment (t=0h).

4. **Measurements:**
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth rate and viability.
   - At the end of 14 hours, measure intracellular glutamate concentrations using mass spectrometry.
   - Assess cell viability post-actinomycin D treatment by comparing growth rates across different glutamate supplementation levels.

5. **Control Experiments:**
   - Measure growth without actinomycin D for each glutamate supplementation level to assess the independent effects of glutamate on growth.
   - Measure growth with actinomycin D but without glutamate supplementation to confirm the baseline resistance of the wild-type strain.

**Expected Outcomes:**

1. **Verification of Glutamate Levels:**
   - Mass spectrometry should show increased intracellular glutamate levels in the low and high glutamate groups compared to the control.

   - In the presence of actinomycin D, the control group should maintain higher growth rates compared to the low and high glutamate groups.
   - If the hypothesis is correct, the high glutamate group will show the most significant decrease in growth rate, indicating decreased resistance to actinomycin D.

3. **Independent Effects:**
   - The control experiment without actinomycin D should show no significant differences in growth rates across different glutamate levels, ensuring that any observed effects in the main experiment are due to the interaction between glutamate levels and actinomycin D treatment.
