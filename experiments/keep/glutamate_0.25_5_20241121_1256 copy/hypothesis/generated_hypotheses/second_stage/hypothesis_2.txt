### Motivation

The statement suggests that higher-than-normal levels of intracellular glutamate are associated with decreased resistance to formic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association could reveal insights into the cellular mechanisms of chemical resistance and the role of glutamate in stress response.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular glutamate levels exhibit decreased resistance to formic acid due to an imbalance in glutamate metabolism leading to altered cellular defenses. It is hypothesized that glutamate may interfere with the detoxification processes or stress response pathways that are critical for resistance to formic acid.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Culture Preparation:** 
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow all cultures in minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Glutamate Supplementation:**
   - Prepare three different concentrations of L-glutamate supplementation: 
     - Control: 0 mM (no supplementation).
     - Low: 5 mM L-glutamate.
     - High: 10 mM L-glutamate.
   - Add glutamate at t=0h for each condition.

3. **Formic Acid Challenge:**
   - At the start of the experiment (t=0h), add formic acid to each well at a standardized concentration (e.g., 10 mM) to challenge the cells' chemical resistance.

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Each condition (glutamate concentration) should be tested in triplicate within a 96-well plate.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for growth rate analysis.
   - Terminate the experiment at 14 hours.

5. **Controls:**
   - Include wells with no formic acid to assess the independent effect of glutamate on growth.
   - Include wells with formic acid but no glutamate supplementation to serve as a baseline for resistance.

6. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of the experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate concentrations and verify the effectiveness of supplementation.

### Expected Outcomes

1. **Growth Rate and Viability:**
   - If the hypothesis is correct, cells with increased intracellular glutamate will show decreased growth rates and viability in the presence of formic acid compared to the control group.
   - Increased glutamate concentrations (5 mM and 10 mM) should correlate with further decreased resistance.

2. **Metabolomics Verification:**
   - Metabolomics analysis should confirm higher intracellular glutamate levels in supplemented groups compared to controls.

3. **Independent Effects:**
   - In wells without formic acid, it is expected that the glutamate supplementation will not significantly affect growth, confirming that the observed effects are specific to the formic acid challenge.

By comparing these outcomes, we can support or refute the association between elevated glutamate levels and decreased resistance to formic acid, while accounting for any independent effects of glutamate supplementation on the phenotype.
