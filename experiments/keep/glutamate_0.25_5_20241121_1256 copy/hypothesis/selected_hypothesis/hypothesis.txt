**Motivation:**

The statement suggests that higher intracellular glutamate levels are associated with decreased resistance to formic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association could provide insights into cellular metabolism and stress response mechanisms.

**Hypothesis:**

Increased intracellular glutamate concentrations reduce the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to formic acid, potentially due to altered metabolic fluxes that affect detoxification pathways or stress response mechanisms.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for all experiments.

2. **Supplementation to Modulate Intracellular Glutamate:**
   - Control: No supplementation.
   - Low Glutamate: Add 5 mM L-glutamate to the media.
   - High Glutamate: Add 15 mM L-glutamate to the media.

3. **Formic Acid Challenge:**
   - Control: No formic acid.
   - Low Formic Acid: Add 0.5% (v/v) formic acid to the media.
   - High Formic Acid: Add 1% (v/v) formic acid to the media.

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Prepare 9 different conditions combining glutamate levels and formic acid concentrations:
     1. Control (No glutamate, no formic acid)
     2. Low Glutamate, No Formic Acid
     3. High Glutamate, No Formic Acid
     4. Control, Low Formic Acid
     5. Low Glutamate, Low Formic Acid
     6. High Glutamate, Low Formic Acid
     7. Control, High Formic Acid
     8. Low Glutamate, High Formic Acid
     9. High Glutamate, High Formic Acid

5. **Inoculation and Growth:**
   - Inoculate each well at 0.05 OD600.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

6. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of 14 hours, harvest cells for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels.

7. **Control Experiments:**
   - Verify the impact of glutamate supplementation on intracellular glutamate levels without formic acid stress.
   - Assess the baseline resistance to formic acid without glutamate supplementation.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Increased Intracellular Glutamate Levels:**
  - Expect higher intracellular glutamate concentrations in the low and high glutamate supplementation conditions compared to the control.
  
- **Growth and Resistance Patterns:**
  - Control conditions (no glutamate, no formic acid) should show normal growth.
  - With formic acid, expect decreased growth rates due to stress.
  - Increased glutamate levels (especially high glutamate) may further decrease growth rates in the presence of formic acid, supporting the hypothesis.
  - If glutamate supplementation independently affects growth without formic acid, it could indicate a direct metabolic impact of glutamate.

- **Verification of Assumptions:**
  - Confirm that glutamate supplementation effectively raises intracellular glutamate levels.
  - Establish that formic acid decreases growth, indicating stress.

This experimental design will help clarify the relationship between intracellular glutamate levels and formic acid resistance, while controlling for independent effects of glutamate supplementation.
