####### OPTION 1 #######

**Motivation:**

The statement suggests that higher intracellular glutamate levels are associated with decreased resistance to formic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association could provide insights into cellular metabolism and stress response mechanisms.

**Hypothesis:**

Increased intracellular glutamate concentrations reduce the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to formic acid, potentially due to altered metabolic fluxes that affect detoxification pathways or stress response mechanisms.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for all experiments.

2. **Supplementation to Modulate Intracellular Glutamate:**
   - Control: No supplementation.
   - Low Glutamate: Add 5 mM L-glutamate to the media.
   - High Glutamate: Add 15 mM L-glutamate to the media.

3. **Formic Acid Challenge:**
   - Control: No formic acid.
   - Low Formic Acid: Add 0.5% (v/v) formic acid to the media.
   - High Formic Acid: Add 1% (v/v) formic acid to the media.

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Prepare 9 different conditions combining glutamate levels and formic acid concentrations:
     1. Control (No glutamate, no formic acid)
     2. Low Glutamate, No Formic Acid
     3. High Glutamate, No Formic Acid
     4. Control, Low Formic Acid
     5. Low Glutamate, Low Formic Acid
     6. High Glutamate, Low Formic Acid
     7. Control, High Formic Acid
     8. Low Glutamate, High Formic Acid
     9. High Glutamate, High Formic Acid

5. **Inoculation and Growth:**
   - Inoculate each well at 0.05 OD600.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

6. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of 14 hours, harvest cells for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate levels.

7. **Control Experiments:**
   - Verify the impact of glutamate supplementation on intracellular glutamate levels without formic acid stress.
   - Assess the baseline resistance to formic acid without glutamate supplementation.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Increased Intracellular Glutamate Levels:**
  - Expect higher intracellular glutamate concentrations in the low and high glutamate supplementation conditions compared to the control.
  
- **Growth and Resistance Patterns:**
  - Control conditions (no glutamate, no formic acid) should show normal growth.
  - With formic acid, expect decreased growth rates due to stress.
  - Increased glutamate levels (especially high glutamate) may further decrease growth rates in the presence of formic acid, supporting the hypothesis.
  - If glutamate supplementation independently affects growth without formic acid, it could indicate a direct metabolic impact of glutamate.

- **Verification of Assumptions:**
  - Confirm that glutamate supplementation effectively raises intracellular glutamate levels.
  - Establish that formic acid decreases growth, indicating stress.

This experimental design will help clarify the relationship between intracellular glutamate levels and formic acid resistance, while controlling for independent effects of glutamate supplementation.

   - Terminate the experiment at 14 hours.

5. **Controls:**

   - Include wells with formic acid but no glutamate supplementation to serve as a baseline for resistance.

3. **Independent Effects:**

####### OPTION 3 #######
**Motivation:**

The aim of this experiment is to investigate the association between elevated intracellular glutamate levels and decreased resistance to formic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic pathways and resistance mechanisms involving glutamate.

**Hypothesis:**

Cells with increased intracellular glutamate levels, as compared to normal levels, exhibit decreased resistance to formic acid. The potential mechanism could be that elevated glutamate levels disrupt the cellular homeostasis or stress response pathways, leading to a compromised ability to cope with formic acid exposure.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Culture Conditions:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in 96-well plates with minimal media without amino acids at 30°C with shaking.
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.

2. **Experimental Setup:**
   - **Control Group:** No glutamate supplementation, mimicking normal intracellular levels.
   - **Low Glutamate Supplementation:** Add 10 mM glutamate to the media to modestly increase intracellular levels.
   - **High Glutamate Supplementation:** Add 30 mM glutamate to the media to significantly increase intracellular levels.

3. **Chemical Resistance Assay:**
   - At t=0h, add formic acid to each well to a final concentration of 5 mM.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth over 14 hours.

4. **Metabolomics:**
   - At experiment termination (14 hours), harvest cells for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular glutamate concentrations.

5. **Controls and Assumption Testing:**
   - Include wells with only formic acid (no glutamate supplementation) to verify its impact independently.
   - Include wells with only glutamate (without formic acid) to assess independent impacts on growth and metabolism.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Control Group:** Cells grow normally with standard resistance to formic acid, confirming baseline resistance levels.
- **Low Glutamate Supplementation:** If cells show decreased growth in the presence of formic acid and elevated intracellular glutamate, it supports the hypothesis that increased glutamate leads to decreased resistance.
- **High Glutamate Supplementation:** A more pronounced decrease in growth compared to the control group would further substantiate the hypothesis.
- **Metabolomics Results:** Intracellular glutamate levels should correlate with supplementation levels, confirming effective modulation.
- **Independent Effects:** 
  - If growth is affected in wells with only glutamate (without formic acid), it suggests a pleiotropic effect, requiring careful interpretation.
  - Any decrease in growth in the presence of formic acid alone (without glutamate) would serve as a baseline for resistance levels.

By comparing growth rates in all conditions, we can conclude whether elevated intracellular glutamate levels are associated with decreased resistance to formic acid, as proposed.
