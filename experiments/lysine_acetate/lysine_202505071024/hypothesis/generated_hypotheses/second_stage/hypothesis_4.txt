### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. This experiment aims to explore whether increasing intracellular lysine levels in yeast cells influences their resistance to acetic acid, a common stressor in yeast fermentation environments.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will decrease the cells' resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine's role in metabolic pathways that affect stress response mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Lysine Supplementation:**
   - Three levels of lysine supplementation:
     - Control: 0 mM lysine
     - Physiological concentration: 1 mM lysine
     - Supraphysiological concentration: 10 mM lysine

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Two levels of acetic acid treatment:
     - Control: 0% acetic acid
     - Treatment: 0.5% acetic acid (a concentration known to induce stress in yeast cells)

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with threonine supplementation (10 mM) instead of lysine, as threonine is not directly related to the hypothesized mechanism. This ensures that any observed effects are specific to lysine.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total conditions: 3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + 2 threonine control conditions = 8 conditions
   - Each condition will be prepared in a separate well of a 96-well plate.
   - All supplementations and treatments will be added at t=0h.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At the end of the 20-hour period, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Lysine Supplementation:**
  - Increased intracellular lysine levels are expected with higher lysine supplementation.
  - Growth curves may show reduced growth in the presence of higher lysine and acetic acid, supporting the hypothesis.

- **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
  - In conditions with 0.5% acetic acid, cells with higher lysine levels may show decreased growth compared to controls, indicating reduced resistance.
  - The threonine control should not show significant changes in resistance to acetic acid, confirming the specificity of lysine's effect.

- **Overall:**
  - If higher lysine levels result in decreased resistance to acetic acid, it would support the association in the statement.
  - Independent effects of lysine supplementation on growth and resistance will be considered to ensure observed outcomes are not due to confounding factors.
