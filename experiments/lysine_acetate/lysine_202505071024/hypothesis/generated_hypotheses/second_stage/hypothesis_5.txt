### Motivation

The statement suggests that there is an association between higher intracellular levels of lysine and a decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship is important for elucidating how amino acid metabolism might influence the yeast's response to environmental stresses, such as exposure to acetic acid, which is a common stressor in industrial fermentation processes.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular lysine levels will result in decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. This may be due to lysine interfering with cellular mechanisms that confer resistance to acidic environments, potentially by affecting membrane integrity or the expression of stress response genes.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids for baseline conditions.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - **Lysine Supplementation:**
     - Control: No lysine supplementation.
     - Level 1: 10 mM lysine (physiological concentration).
     - Level 2: 50 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a negative control with 50 mM threonine (relevant as it is not directly involved in the lysine metabolic pathway).

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Set up a 96-well plate with the following combinations:
     - Lysine Control + Acetic Acid Control
     - Lysine Control + Acetic Acid Treatment
     - Lysine Level 1 + Acetic Acid Control
     - Lysine Level 1 + Acetic Acid Treatment
     - Lysine Level 2 + Acetic Acid Control
     - Lysine Level 2 + Acetic Acid Treatment
     - Threonine Control + Acetic Acid Control
     - Threonine Control + Acetic Acid Treatment

6. **Procedure:**
   - Inoculate each well with the yeast strain and incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Add lysine or threonine supplementation and acetic acid treatment at t=0h.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - After 20 hours, terminate the experiment and collect samples for intracellular metabolite analysis using mass spectrometry-based metabolomics.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Lysine Supplementation Effects:**
  - If higher intracellular lysine levels are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see reduced growth (lower OD600) in wells with higher lysine supplementation when exposed to acetic acid compared to controls.
  - Mass spectrometry should confirm increased intracellular lysine levels in the supplemented conditions.

- **Threonine Control:**
  - The threonine control should show no significant difference in growth between acetic acid treated and untreated conditions, indicating that threonine does not affect acetic acid resistance.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - Any observed decrease in growth in the presence of acetic acid and lysine should be attributed to the interaction between lysine levels and acetic acid stress, rather than an independent effect of lysine on growth.

This experimental setup will help determine if there is indeed an association between intracellular lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, and can provide insights into the underlying mechanisms of this interaction.
