### Motivation

The goal of this experiment is to investigate whether increased intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid. Acetic acid is a common stressor in yeast fermentation processes, and understanding how lysine levels affect yeast resistance to such chemicals could provide insights into yeast stress responses and metabolic regulation.

### Hypothesis

Higher intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine's role in metabolic pathways that influence stress response mechanisms or membrane integrity.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Supplementation Levels**
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: Supplement with 1 mM lysine.
   - High Lysine: Supplement with 10 mM lysine (supraphysiological level).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid, a concentration known to induce stress in yeast.

4. **Negative Control**
   - Use threonine as a negative control at 10 mM, equimolar to the highest lysine supplementation, to ensure that the effects observed are specific to lysine.

5. **Experimental Conditions**
   - For each lysine level (0 mM, 1 mM, 10 mM), test with and without 50 mM acetic acid.
   - Include threonine controls with and without 50 mM acetic acid.
   - Total conditions: 3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + 2 threonine controls = 8 conditions.

6. **Measurements**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At the end of 20 hours, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to determine intracellular lysine levels.
   - Assess viability and growth curves to evaluate resistance to acetic acid.

### Expected Outcomes

- If higher intracellular lysine levels decrease resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see reduced growth and viability in the high lysine + acetic acid condition compared to the control and low lysine + acetic acid conditions.
- The threonine control should not show a similar decrease in resistance, confirming the specificity of lysine's effect.
- Mass spectrometry should verify increased intracellular lysine levels in the supplemented conditions, confirming effective modulation.

This experimental design will help elucidate whether lysine levels specifically impact acetic acid resistance, providing insights into the metabolic and stress response pathways in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
