### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. To explore this association, we will modulate lysine levels and assess the yeast's resistance to acetic acid. This experiment will help us understand whether lysine levels affect the yeast's ability to withstand acetic acid stress.

### Hypothesis
Elevated intracellular lysine levels may interfere with cellular mechanisms that confer resistance to acetic acid, potentially through metabolic or regulatory pathways that are sensitive to lysine concentrations. We hypothesize that increasing lysine levels will decrease the yeast's resistance to acetic acid, as measured by growth and viability in the presence of acetic acid.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Supplementations:**
   - Lysine Supplementation Levels:
     - Control: No lysine
     - Physiological Level: 50 mg/L lysine
     - Supraphysiological Level: 200 mg/L lysine

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Acetic Acid Concentrations:
     - Control: No acetic acid
     - Low Stress: 10 mM acetic acid
     - High Stress: 50 mM acetic acid

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Threonine at 200 mg/L (equimolar concentration to the highest lysine supplementation level).

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Each combination of lysine and acetic acid levels will be tested:
     - 3 lysine levels x 3 acetic acid levels = 9 conditions
     - Plus 2 negative control conditions: threonine with no acetic acid and threonine with high acetic acid.
   - Total conditions: 11

6. **Procedure:**
   - Inoculate yeast cells into each well with the appropriate media and supplementations at t=0h.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

### Expected Outcomes
- If lysine levels are associated with decreased acetic acid resistance, we expect to see reduced growth and viability in conditions with higher lysine supplementation when exposed to acetic acid.
- The negative control with threonine should not show decreased resistance to acetic acid, confirming that the observed effects are specific to lysine.
- Mass spectrometry should confirm elevated intracellular lysine levels in supplemented conditions, verifying successful modulation.

This experimental design will help determine if there is a correlation between lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, providing insights into the metabolic or regulatory pathways involved.
