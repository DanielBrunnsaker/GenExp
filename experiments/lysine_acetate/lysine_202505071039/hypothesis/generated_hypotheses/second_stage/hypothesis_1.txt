**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association can provide insights into lysine's role in cellular stress responses and its potential impact on yeast's ability to withstand acetic acid, a common stressor in industrial fermentation processes.

**Hypothesis:**
Elevated intracellular lysine levels may interfere with cellular mechanisms that confer resistance to acetic acid, potentially through modulation of membrane integrity or stress response pathways, resulting in decreased resistance to acetic acid.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids unless specified otherwise.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Lysine supplementation at two levels:
     - Control: No lysine supplementation.
     - Physiological Level: 0.1 mM lysine.
     - Supraphysiological Level: 10 mM lysine.
   - Negative Control: Use leucine at 10 mM as a negative control, as it is not directly involved in acetic acid resistance pathways.

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 20 mM acetic acid, added at t=0h.

4. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total conditions: (2 lysine levels + 1 control) x (2 acetic acid levels) + (2 negative control conditions) = 8 conditions.
   - Incubate all conditions at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and conduct mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Condition 1: Control (no lysine, no acetic acid)
   - Condition 2: Physiological lysine (0.1 mM, no acetic acid)
   - Condition 3: Supraphysiological lysine (10 mM, no acetic acid)
   - Condition 4: Control (no lysine, 20 mM acetic acid)
   - Condition 5: Physiological lysine (0.1 mM, 20 mM acetic acid)
   - Condition 6: Supraphysiological lysine (10 mM, 20 mM acetic acid)
   - Condition 7: Negative control (10 mM leucine, no acetic acid)
   - Condition 8: Negative control (10 mM leucine, 20 mM acetic acid)

**Expected Outcomes:**
- Conditions with increased lysine supplementation (especially supraphysiological) may show decreased growth or viability in the presence of acetic acid, compared to the control and physiological lysine levels, supporting the hypothesis.
- The negative control (leucine) should not exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid, confirming that the observed effects are specific to lysine.
- Mass spectrometry results should confirm increased intracellular lysine levels in supplemented conditions, validating the modulation of lysine levels.
- The results will help determine if there is a correlation between lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, providing insights into the potential mechanisms underlying this association.
