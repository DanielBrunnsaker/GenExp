### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into how amino acid metabolism influences stress responses, particularly in environments containing acetic acid, a common stressor in industrial fermentation processes.

### Hypothesis
Higher intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may interfere with cellular mechanisms that confer resistance to acetic acid, potentially through modulation of stress response pathways or membrane integrity.

### Experimental Plan

#### Experimental Setup
1. **Strain and Media**:
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Baseline media: Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Lysine Supplementation**:
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low lysine: 1 mM lysine.
   - High lysine: 10 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment**:
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treated: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use threonine at 10 mM as a negative control to ensure that any observed effects are specific to lysine and not general amino acid supplementation.

#### Experimental Conditions
- Total combinations: 3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid conditions = 6 conditions, plus 2 negative control conditions (lysine and threonine without acetic acid).
- Each condition will be set up in triplicate wells in a 96-well plate.
- Incubation: 30°C with shaking.
- Supplementations and treatments are added at t=0h.

#### Measurements
- Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.
- At 20h, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels and verify modulation.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Control Conditions**: Cells with no lysine supplementation should exhibit baseline resistance to acetic acid.
- **Low Lysine Supplementation**: Cells may show slight changes in resistance to acetic acid, depending on the degree of lysine's impact on stress response pathways.
- **High Lysine Supplementation**: If higher lysine levels are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see a significant reduction in growth or viability in the presence of acetic acid compared to controls.
- **Negative Control**: Threonine supplementation should not affect resistance to acetic acid, confirming lysine-specific effects.

By comparing growth curves and intracellular metabolite levels across conditions, we can determine if higher intracellular lysine correlates with decreased resistance to acetic acid. This design minimizes confounders and tests the hypothesis with appropriate controls.
