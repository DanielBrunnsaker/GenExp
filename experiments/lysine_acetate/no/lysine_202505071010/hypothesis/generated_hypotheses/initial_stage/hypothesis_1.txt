### Motivation

Lysine is an essential amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways and cellular functions in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding the relationship between intracellular lysine levels and resistance to chemicals, such as acetic acid, could provide insights into yeast metabolism and stress responses. This experiment aims to explore whether increased intracellular lysine levels correlate with decreased resistance to acetic acid.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid. This could be due to lysine's role in metabolic pathways that affect stress response mechanisms, potentially altering membrane integrity or cellular detoxification processes.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae. Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Lysine Supplementation Levels**:
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: 0.5 mM lysine.
   - High Lysine: 5 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels**:
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treated: 20 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use threonine at 5 mM (equimolar to the highest lysine concentration) as a negative control to ensure that any observed effects are specific to lysine.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Prepare 96-well plates with combinations of lysine supplementation and acetic acid treatment.
   - Each condition should be set up in triplicate wells to ensure statistical significance.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

6. **Measurements**:
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

7. **Conditions**:
   - Total conditions: 3 lysine levels × 2 acetic acid levels + 2 negative control conditions = 8 conditions.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions**: Cells without lysine supplementation and without acetic acid treatment should exhibit normal growth, serving as a baseline for comparison.
  
- **Lysine Supplementation**: 
  - Low lysine supplementation may show minimal effects on acetic acid resistance.
  - High lysine supplementation might lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid, observed as reduced growth or viability in the presence of acetic acid.

- **Negative Control**: Threonine supplementation should not affect acetic acid resistance, confirming that the effects are specific to lysine.

- **Mass Spectrometry Results**: Increased intracellular lysine levels should be confirmed in the supplemented conditions, particularly at the high lysine concentration.

This experimental design will help determine if there is an association between increased intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid, while controlling for confounding factors.
