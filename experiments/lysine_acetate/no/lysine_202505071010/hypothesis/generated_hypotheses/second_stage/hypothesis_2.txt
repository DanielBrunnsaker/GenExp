### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between elevated intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship can provide insights into how amino acid levels might influence stress resistance mechanisms, particularly in the context of acetic acid, a common stressor in industrial fermentation processes.

### Hypothesis
Elevated intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid due to potential disruptions in cellular homeostasis or metabolic pathways that are involved in stress response mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Base media: Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Lysine Supplementation:**
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: 50 mg/L lysine.
   - High Lysine: 500 mg/L lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Acetic Acid Treatment: 0.5% (v/v) acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a minimal media condition supplemented with leucine at 500 mg/L to serve as a negative control. This ensures that any observed effects are specific to lysine rather than general amino acid supplementation. 

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Each lysine supplementation level will be tested with and without acetic acid treatment.
   - Total conditions: (3 lysine levels) x (2 acetic acid levels) + 2 negative controls = 8 conditions.
   - Each condition will be set up in a separate well of a 96-well plate.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At the end of the 20-hour experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels and confirm the modulation.
   - Assess viability and growth curves to determine resistance to acetic acid.

### Expected Outcomes
- If elevated intracellular lysine levels are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see reduced growth and viability in conditions with high lysine supplementation when acetic acid is present.
- The negative control with leucine should not exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid, confirming the specificity of the lysine effect.
- The mass spectrometry results should confirm that intracellular lysine levels increase with higher supplementation.

This experimental design will help elucidate the relationship between lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, while controlling for confounding variables through the use of appropriate controls and independent verification of intracellular lysine levels.
