### Motivation

The statement suggests that higher intracellular lysine levels may be associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Lysine is a basic amino acid that might affect cellular processes related to stress response, including resistance to acidic environments. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into how lysine levels influence yeast's ability to withstand chemical stress.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular lysine concentrations in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine's role in modulating stress response pathways or affecting membrane stability under acidic conditions.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain. Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Lysine Supplementation Levels**:
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: 0.1 mM lysine.
   - High Lysine: 10 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment**:
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 50 mM acetic acid.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use threonine as a negative control. Supplement with 10 mM threonine (equimolar to the highest lysine concentration).

5. **Experimental Conditions**:
   - Set up 96-well plates with the following combinations:
     - No lysine, no acetic acid.
     - 0.1 mM lysine, no acetic acid.
     - 10 mM lysine, no acetic acid.
     - No lysine, 50 mM acetic acid.
     - 0.1 mM lysine, 50 mM acetic acid.
     - 10 mM lysine, 50 mM acetic acid.
     - 10 mM threonine, no acetic acid.
     - 10 mM threonine, 50 mM acetic acid.

6. **Experimental Protocol**:
   - Inoculate each well with standardized yeast culture.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels at the end of the experiment.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Growth Curves**: 
  - Wells with acetic acid are expected to show decreased growth compared to those without.
  - Wells with higher lysine concentrations (10 mM) may exhibit further decreased growth in the presence of acetic acid, supporting the hypothesis.
  
- **Intracellular Lysine Levels**:
  - Mass spectrometry should confirm increased lysine levels in supplemented conditions.

- **Threonine Control**:
  - Threonine-supplemented conditions should not show a significant change in acetic acid resistance, validating its role as a negative control.

This experimental setup will help discern whether increased lysine levels correlate with decreased resistance to acetic acid, while controlling for potential confounders.
