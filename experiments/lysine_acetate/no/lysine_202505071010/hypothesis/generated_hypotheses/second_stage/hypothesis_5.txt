### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into how lysine metabolism affects yeast's stress response mechanisms, particularly towards organic acids like acetic acid, which are common stressors in industrial fermentation processes.

### Hypothesis
Elevated intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae lead to a decreased resistance to acetic acid due to potential disruptions in cellular homeostasis or metabolic pathways that are sensitive to lysine concentrations.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Baseline Conditions:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the base medium.

2. **Lysine Supplementation:**
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: 1 mM lysine.
   - High Lysine: 10 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Acetic Acid: 50 mM acetic acid, a concentration known to induce stress in yeast.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use threonine at 10 mM as a negative control, ensuring it is equimolar to the highest lysine concentration. Threonine should not directly influence the pathways associated with acetic acid resistance.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Set up a 96-well plate with the following conditions (3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + negative control):
     - Lysine (0, 1, 10 mM) x Acetic Acid (0, 50 mM)
     - Threonine 10 mM with and without 50 mM acetic acid.

6. **Procedure:**
   - Inoculate each well with a standardized concentration of yeast cells.
   - Incubate with shaking at 30°C.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine concentrations at the end of the experiment.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Growth Curves:**
  - In the absence of acetic acid, we expect normal growth across all lysine levels.
  - In the presence of acetic acid, we anticipate reduced growth, particularly at higher lysine levels, if the hypothesis is correct.

- **Intracellular Lysine Levels:**
  - Mass spectrometry should confirm elevated intracellular lysine in the supplemented conditions.

- **Interpretation:**
  - If higher lysine levels correlate with reduced growth or viability in the presence of acetic acid, it would support the hypothesis that lysine affects resistance mechanisms.
  - The negative control with threonine should not show a decrease in resistance, indicating that the effect is specific to lysine and not a general amino acid effect.

This experiment will help determine if there is a mechanistic link between lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, potentially guiding further studies on metabolic regulation and stress resistance in yeast.
