**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship is important for elucidating how amino acid metabolism impacts stress resistance, which could have implications for industrial fermentation processes where acetic acid is a common stressor.

**Hypothesis:**
Higher intracellular lysine concentrations in Saccharomyces cerevisiae lead to decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine's role in metabolic pathways that influence stress response mechanisms.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Media Preparation and Controls:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
   - Include a negative control with leucine supplementation at the highest lysine concentration used to ensure that the observed effects are specific to lysine and not due to general amino acid supplementation.

2. **Lysine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No lysine supplementation.
   - Level 2: Physiological lysine concentration (50 mg/L).
   - Level 3: Supraphysiological lysine concentration (500 mg/L).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No acetic acid.
   - Level 2: Acetic acid concentration at 0.5% (v/v), a common stress-inducing concentration for yeast.

4. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Set up a 96-well plate with each well containing a different combination of lysine supplementation and acetic acid treatment. Each condition will have multiple replicates.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

5. **Variable Combinations:**
   - 3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid levels = 6 conditions.
   - Include treated and untreated leucine negative control = 2 additional conditions.
   - Total conditions: 8

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If higher lysine levels are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see reduced growth in wells with higher lysine concentrations when exposed to acetic acid.
- The negative control with leucine should not show decreased resistance, confirming that the effect is specific to lysine.
- Mass spectrometry should confirm increased intracellular lysine levels in the supplemented conditions.
- The experiment will help determine if the association holds true under controlled conditions and provide insights into the role of lysine in stress resistance.
