### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic pathways influencing chemical resistance and the role of amino acid homeostasis in stress responses.

### Hypothesis
Elevated intracellular lysine levels may interfere with metabolic pathways or regulatory networks that modulate the yeast's resistance to acetic acid, possibly by impacting the cell wall integrity or stress response pathways.

### Experimental Plan

#### Media and Baseline Conditions
- Use minimal media without amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.
- Maintain all cultures at 30°C with constant shaking.

#### Supplementation Levels
1. **Control**: No lysine supplementation.
2. **Lysine Low**: 0.5 mM lysine.
3. **Lysine High**: 5 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

#### Treatment Levels
1. **Control**: No acetic acid.
2. **Acetic Acid**: 50 mM acetic acid.

#### Negative Control
- Use threonine as a negative control at 5 mM, equimolar to the highest lysine supplementation, as it is not directly involved in acetic acid resistance pathways.

#### Experimental Conditions
- Total conditions: 3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + 2 negative controls = 8 conditions.
- Each condition should be run in biological triplicates for robustness.

#### Steps
1. Prepare 96-well plates with the designated media and supplementation.
2. Inoculate each well with the reference strain of S. cerevisiae.
3. Add lysine or threonine to the appropriate wells at t=0h.
4. Introduce acetic acid to the designated wells at t=0h.
5. Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and viability.
6. At 20 hours, harvest cells for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine concentrations.

### Expected Outcomes
- **If the hypothesis is correct**: Wells with higher lysine levels (5 mM) will show decreased growth in the presence of acetic acid compared to controls, indicating decreased resistance. This would suggest that elevated lysine levels correlate with increased sensitivity to acetic acid.
- **Independent Effects**: Lysine supplementation alone may affect growth due to its role in protein synthesis and metabolism. The acetic acid treatment alone should reduce growth due to its known toxicity.
- **Negative Control**: Threonine supplementation should not affect acetic acid resistance, serving as a baseline for normal resistance levels.

This experiment will help determine if there is a causal relationship between lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, beyond mere association.
