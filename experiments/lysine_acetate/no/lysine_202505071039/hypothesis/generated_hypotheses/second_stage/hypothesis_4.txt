### Motivation
The statement suggests that higher intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid. Acetic acid is a common stressor in yeast fermentation processes, and understanding how lysine levels influence resistance could provide insights into yeast metabolism and stress response.

### Hypothesis
Increasing intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will lead to a decrease in resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine's role in cellular metabolism or stress response pathways that interact with acetic acid resistance mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

#### Media and Baseline Conditions
- Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
- Maintain standard temperature (30°C) and shaking conditions.

#### Supplementation Levels
- **Lysine Supplementation**: 
  - Control: No lysine supplementation.
  - Low: 10 mM lysine.
  - High: 50 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

#### Treatment Levels
- **Acetic Acid Exposure**:
  - Control: No acetic acid.
  - Treatment: 20 mM acetic acid.

#### Negative Control
- **Leucine as Negative Control**:
  - Control: 50 mM leucine (equimolar to the highest lysine concentration).

#### Experimental Setup
- Total Conditions: 3 lysine levels x 2 acetic acid levels + 2 leucine controls = 8 conditions.
- Each condition will be set up in a 96-well plate format with appropriate replicates.

#### Procedure
1. Prepare minimal media and supplement with lysine at 0 mM, 10 mM, and 50 mM concentrations.
2. Add acetic acid to half of the wells to achieve 20 mM concentration at t=0h.
3. Include leucine controls with 50 mM leucine with and without acetic acid.
4. Inoculate wells with a standardized concentration of wild-type S. cerevisiae.
5. Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
6. At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Lysine Supplementation Effects**:
  - Higher intracellular lysine levels are expected in wells with 50 mM lysine supplementation.
  - Growth curves will indicate the impact of lysine on growth and viability.

- **Acetic Acid Resistance**:
  - If the hypothesis is correct, wells with higher lysine supplementation will show decreased growth in the presence of acetic acid compared to controls.
  - The leucine control should not show a decrease in resistance to acetic acid, confirming lysine-specific effects.

- **Independent Effects**:
  - Any observed changes in growth or resistance should be attributed to lysine levels rather than other confounding factors, as verified by the leucine negative control.

This experimental setup will help determine if there is a correlation between intracellular lysine levels and acetic acid resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
