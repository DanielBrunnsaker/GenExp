**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to acetic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this relationship is important because it could reveal insights into how lysine metabolism influences yeast cell resilience to acidic environments, which is relevant for industrial fermentation processes.

**Hypothesis:**
Elevated intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae under standard conditions are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine interacting with pathways that modulate stress response mechanisms.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure that any lysine present is due to supplementation.

2. **Lysine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control (0 mM lysine)
   - Physiological concentration (1 mM lysine)
   - Supraphysiological concentration (10 mM lysine)

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control (0% acetic acid)
   - Treatment (0.5% acetic acid), a concentration known to stress yeast cells but allow for survival.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Include a minimal media condition supplemented with 10 mM leucine, serving as a negative control to ensure that the observed effects are specific to lysine and not due to general amino acid supplementation.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Prepare 96-well plates with each combination of lysine supplementation and acetic acid treatment (2 lysine levels + control) x (1 acetic acid level + control) = 6 conditions.
   - Include wells for the negative control (10 mM leucine with and without acetic acid).

6. **Incubation:**
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking to ensure aeration and homogeneity.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.

7. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine concentrations and verify modulation.

8. **Verification of Assumptions:**
   - Ensure that lysine supplementation indeed results in elevated intracellular lysine levels through metabolomics.
   - Confirm that leucine does not affect acetic acid resistance under the experimental conditions.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Lysine Supplementation Effects:**
  - Higher intracellular lysine levels are expected in the 1 mM and 10 mM lysine conditions compared to the control.
  - If the hypothesis is correct, cells with higher lysine levels will exhibit decreased growth or viability in the presence of acetic acid compared to controls.

- **Acetic Acid Resistance:**
  - In the presence of acetic acid, cells with elevated lysine (especially at 10 mM) might show decreased growth rates or lower final OD600 compared to the control, indicating decreased resistance.

- **Negative Control:**
  - The leucine-supplemented conditions should not show decreased resistance to acetic acid, confirming the specificity of lysine's effect.

This experimental design aims to elucidate the relationship between lysine levels and acetic acid resistance, while controlling for confounding variables and verifying assumptions.
