### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular levels of histidine and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is an amino acid that can play roles in metal ion binding and homeostasis, potentially influencing cellular resistance to metal-induced stress. This experiment aims to evaluate whether modulating intracellular histidine levels affects yeast resistance to zinc dichloride.

### Hypothesis
Reducing intracellular histidine levels will decrease the resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae to zinc dichloride due to histidine's potential role in metal ion buffering and detoxification processes.

### Experimental Plan
1. **Baseline Conditions**: Use minimal media without amino acids as the base media.
   
2. **Histidine Modulation**:
   - **Control**: No histidine supplementation.
   - **Low Histidine**: Supplement with 0.1 mM histidine to potentially reduce intracellular histidine levels below physiological levels.
   - **High Histidine**: Supplement with 5 mM histidine to achieve supraphysiological levels.

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment**:
   - **Control**: No zinc dichloride treatment.
   - **Treated**: Add 1 mM zinc dichloride to test resistance.

4. **Negative Control**: Use 5 mM lysine as a negative control, as it is not directly involved in metal ion buffering and should not influence zinc dichloride resistance.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Combine each histidine level with both zinc dichloride conditions (untreated and treated), resulting in 6 main conditions.
   - Include the negative control with and without zinc dichloride treatment.
   - Total conditions: 8 (3 histidine levels x 2 zinc dichloride levels + 2 negative control conditions).

6. **Measurements**:
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At 20 hours, measure intracellular histidine levels and any changes in metabolite profiles using mass spectrometry-based metabolomics.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Low Histidine with Zinc Dichloride**: Expect reduced growth compared to control, indicating decreased resistance due to lower histidine levels.
- **High Histidine with Zinc Dichloride**: Potentially observe increased resistance compared to control, suggesting a protective effect of higher histidine levels.
- **Negative Control**: No significant change in resistance to zinc dichloride, confirming the specificity of histidine's role in modulating resistance.
- **Independent Effects**: Ensure that any observed effects are due to histidine modulation and not other confounding factors by comparing growth and metabolite profiles across conditions.
