### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between lower intracellular histidine levels and decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Histidine is known to play a role in metal ion homeostasis and could potentially influence the cell's ability to resist metal-induced stress. Understanding this association could provide insights into the mechanisms of metal resistance and the role of histidine in cellular defense systems.

### Hypothesis

Lower intracellular histidine levels result in decreased resistance to zinc dichloride in Saccharomyces cerevisiae by impairing the cell's ability to maintain metal ion homeostasis, possibly due to histidine's role in chelating metal ions and protecting cellular components from metal-induced damage.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Baseline Conditions**: Use minimal media without amino acids to ensure that any histidine present is due to supplementation.

2. **Histidine Supplementation**:
   - Control: No histidine supplementation.
   - Low Histidine: 0.1 mM histidine.
   - High Histidine: 1 mM histidine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment**:
   - Control: No zinc dichloride.
   - Zinc Treatment: 0.5 mM zinc dichloride (a relevant concentration for stress induction).

4. **Negative Control**: Include a condition with 0.5 mM lysine supplementation as a negative control, as lysine is not directly involved in metal ion homeostasis or resistance.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Prepare 96-well plates with the following combinations of conditions:
     - Histidine Control + Zinc Control
     - Histidine Control + Zinc Treatment
     - Low Histidine + Zinc Control
     - Low Histidine + Zinc Treatment
     - High Histidine + Zinc Control
     - High Histidine + Zinc Treatment
     - Lysine Control + Zinc Control
     - Lysine Control + Zinc Treatment

6. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular histidine levels and confirm modulation.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions**: Cells without zinc treatment should grow normally, with growth rates increasing with higher histidine supplementation.
- **Zinc Treatment**: 
  - In the absence of histidine, cells are expected to show decreased growth and viability, indicating decreased resistance to zinc dichloride.
  - Low histidine supplementation may show intermediate resistance, with some growth impairment.
  - High histidine supplementation is expected to confer increased resistance, with better growth and viability compared to lower histidine levels.
- **Lysine Negative Control**: Should not significantly alter zinc resistance, confirming that any observed effects are specific to histidine.

This experiment will help determine if increased histidine levels can mitigate the effects of zinc dichloride, supporting the association between histidine levels and chemical resistance.
