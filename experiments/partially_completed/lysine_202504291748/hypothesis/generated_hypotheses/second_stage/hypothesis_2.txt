### Motivation

The statement suggests that higher intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may be associated with decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress when exposed to media containing 30% (w/v) sucrose. This experiment seeks to explore the relationship between lysine levels and the yeast's ability to withstand hyperosmotic stress, potentially revealing insights into lysine's role in stress response mechanisms.

### Hypothesis

Increased intracellular lysine concentration in S. cerevisiae correlates with decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress induced by 30% (w/v) sucrose. This may be due to lysine's impact on cellular osmoregulation pathways or its influence on other stress response mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: Use a wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae. Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the base.

2. **Lysine Supplementation**:
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low Lysine: Supplement with 0.5 mM lysine.
   - High Lysine: Supplement with 5 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Hyperosmotic Stress Condition**:
   - All wells will be exposed to media containing 30% (w/v) sucrose at t=0h to induce hyperosmotic stress.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Include a set of wells with 5 mM valine (equimolar to the high lysine condition) without lysine supplementation, to verify that any observed effects are specific to lysine.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - 96-well plate layout:
     - Rows 1-3: Control (no lysine), Low Lysine (0.5 mM), High Lysine (5 mM) with 30% sucrose.
     - Rows 4: Negative control with 5 mM valine and 30% sucrose.
   - Each condition should have sufficient replicates to ensure statistical significance.

6. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and determine viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine concentrations.

7. **Temperature and Shaking**: Incubate at 30°C with shaking to ensure consistent growth conditions.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control**: Baseline growth and stress resistance in 30% sucrose without lysine supplementation.
- **Low Lysine**: Potentially similar growth to control, indicating minimal impact of low lysine on stress resistance.
- **High Lysine**: If hypothesis is correct, expect reduced growth and viability compared to control, indicating decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress.
- **Negative Control (Valine)**: Growth and stress resistance similar to control, confirming that observed effects are specific to lysine.

This design will help determine if increased lysine levels correlate with decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress, providing insights into lysine's role in yeast stress responses.
