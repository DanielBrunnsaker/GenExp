### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular lysine levels and decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, specifically in media containing 30% (w/v) sucrose. Understanding this relationship can provide insights into the role of lysine and osmotic stress response pathways in yeast.

### Hypothesis
Increasing intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae may lead to decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress when exposed to media containing 30% (w/v) sucrose. This could be due to lysine's potential effects on cellular osmotic balance or its role in metabolic pathways that influence stress response.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline media.

2. **Lysine Supplementation:**
   - Prepare three levels of lysine supplementation:
     - Control: 0 mM lysine (no supplementation).
     - Physiological concentration: 2 mM lysine.
     - Supraphysiological concentration: 10 mM lysine.

3. **Hyperosmotic Stress Condition:**
   - Prepare media containing 30% (w/v) sucrose to induce hyperosmotic stress.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use valine as a negative control, supplementing at 10 mM to match the highest lysine concentration.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Allocate wells for each combination of lysine supplementation and stress condition:
     - 3 lysine levels x 2 stress conditions (with and without 30% sucrose) = 6 conditions.
     - Include valine control with and without 30% sucrose = 2 additional conditions.
   - Total conditions: 8.

6. **Incubation:**
   - Inoculate each well with a standardized cell density.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

7. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine concentrations.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Without Sucrose (Standard Conditions):**
  - Lysine supplementation may increase intracellular lysine levels without affecting overall growth in standard conditions.

- **With 30% Sucrose (Hyperosmotic Stress):**
  - If the hypothesis is correct, higher lysine levels (especially at supraphysiological concentrations) could result in decreased growth or viability compared to the control, indicating decreased resistance to hyperosmotic stress.
  - The valine control should not exhibit significant changes in growth or viability under hyperosmotic conditions, confirming its role as a negative control.

The results will help determine if there is a dose-dependent relationship between lysine levels and hyperosmotic stress resistance, supporting or refuting the association suggested in the statement.
