**Motivation:**
The statement suggests that increased intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with decreased resistance to acetic acid. This experiment aims to explore whether manipulating intracellular lysine levels affects the yeast's resistance to acetic acid, potentially unveiling a link between lysine metabolism and stress response mechanisms.

**Hypothesis:**
Increasing intracellular lysine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae reduces the yeast's resistance to acetic acid, potentially due to lysine's influence on cellular stress response pathways or membrane integrity.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic S. cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids, except where lysine supplementation is specified.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control: No lysine supplementation.
   - Low lysine: Supplement with 1 mM lysine.
   - High lysine: Supplement with 10 mM lysine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Acetic Acid Treatment Levels:**
   - Control: No acetic acid.
   - Treatment: 20 mM acetic acid (a concentration known to impose stress on yeast cells).

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use leucine as a negative control to ensure that the observed effects are specific to lysine. Supplement with 10 mM leucine in a parallel set of wells.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Each combination of lysine supplementation and acetic acid treatment will be tested:
     - No lysine, no acetic acid
     - No lysine, 20 mM acetic acid
     - 1 mM lysine, no acetic acid
     - 1 mM lysine, 20 mM acetic acid
     - 10 mM lysine, no acetic acid
     - 10 mM lysine, 20 mM acetic acid
   - Include leucine controls with and without acetic acid:
     - 10 mM leucine, no acetic acid
     - 10 mM leucine, 20 mM acetic acid

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular lysine levels.

7. **Assumptions and Controls:**
   - Verify that lysine supplementation effectively increases intracellular lysine levels through metabolomics.
   - Include appropriate controls to ensure that observed effects are not due to other factors, such as osmotic stress from amino acid supplementation.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- If increased intracellular lysine levels reduce resistance to acetic acid, we expect to see a more significant growth inhibition in the presence of 20 mM acetic acid in the high lysine condition compared to control and low lysine conditions.
- If lysine does not affect resistance, growth inhibition should be similar across all lysine supplementation levels when exposed to acetic acid.
- Leucine controls should not exhibit decreased resistance to acetic acid, confirming lysine's specific role.
- The results will help clarify the relationship between lysine metabolism and chemical stress resistance in S. cerevisiae.
