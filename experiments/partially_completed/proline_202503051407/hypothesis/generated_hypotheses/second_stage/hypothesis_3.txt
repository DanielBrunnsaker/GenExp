### Motivation
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular proline levels and increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Proline is known to act as an osmoprotectant and can contribute to stress resistance, which may include resistance to various chemicals. Investigating this association could provide insights into the role of proline in chemical stress resistance.

### Hypothesis
Increasing intracellular proline levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will enhance its resistance to (S)-lactic acid, potentially by stabilizing proteins and membranes or by acting as a reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenger.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as a baseline.

2. **Proline Supplementation:**
   - Control: No proline supplementation.
   - Low Proline: Supplement with 2 mM L-proline.
   - High Proline: Supplement with 5 mM L-proline.

3. **(S)-Lactic Acid Treatment:**
   - Control: No (S)-lactic acid.
   - Treatment: 10 mM (S)-lactic acid.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Supplement with 5 mM alanine (equimolar to the highest proline concentration) as a negative control, as alanine does not have the same osmoprotectant properties as proline.

5. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Prepare 96-well plates with each well containing one of the following combinations:
     - No proline, No (S)-lactic acid
     - No proline, 10 mM (S)-lactic acid
     - 2 mM proline, No (S)-lactic acid
     - 2 mM proline, 10 mM (S)-lactic acid
     - 5 mM proline, No (S)-lactic acid
     - 5 mM proline, 10 mM (S)-lactic acid
     - 5 mM alanine, No (S)-lactic acid
     - 5 mM alanine, 10 mM (S)-lactic acid

6. **Incubation:**
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking for 20 hours.

7. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - At experiment termination, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular proline levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Proline Levels:** Wells with proline supplementation should show increased intracellular proline levels, confirmed by metabolomics.
  
- **Growth and Viability:**
  - If the hypothesis is correct, wells with higher proline supplementation (5 mM) and (S)-lactic acid treatment should exhibit higher growth rates and viability compared to wells without proline supplementation.
  - Wells with alanine supplementation should not show increased resistance to (S)-lactic acid, serving as a negative control.

- **Dose-Response Relationship:** A positive correlation between proline concentration and resistance to (S)-lactic acid is expected, with higher proline concentrations resulting in greater resistance.

This experimental design will help determine if there is a direct relationship between intracellular proline levels and resistance to (S)-lactic acid, and it will verify if proline supplementation can be a viable method to enhance chemical resistance in yeast.
