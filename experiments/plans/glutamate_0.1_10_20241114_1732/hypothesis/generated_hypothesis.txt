### Selected Statement
Cells with higher than normal levels of intracellular glutamate in standard conditions (grown on minimal media without any amino acids) associate with the following phenotype, described as a prolog program: 
```prolog
Cell(A):-exhibits_phenotype(A,'decreased metal resistance',B,C),compound_name(B,'zinc dichloride').
```

### Motivation
I have selected statement 4 because it presents a specific and testable association between intracellular glutamate levels and metal resistance, specifically to zinc dichloride. This statement is feasible to test due to the availability of zinc dichloride as a treatment and the ability to modulate intracellular glutamate levels through known physiological pathways. Additionally, zinc is a biologically relevant metal, and understanding its interaction with glutamate levels could provide insights into broader metabolic and stress response networks.

### Hypothesis
Increasing intracellular glutamate levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will result in decreased resistance to zinc dichloride, observable through reduced growth rates or viability compared to control conditions.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.

2. **Glutamate Modulation:**
   - Three conditions: 
     - Control: No supplementation.
     - Low Glutamate: 10 mM L-glutamate.
     - High Glutamate: 50 mM L-glutamate.
   - Verify intracellular glutamate levels using mass-spectrometry based metabolomics at the end of the experiment.

3. **Zinc Dichloride Treatment:**
   - Three concentrations of zinc dichloride to evaluate resistance:
     - Control: 0 mM.
     - Moderate: 0.5 mM.
     - High: 1.0 mM.

4. **Experimental Design:**
   - 96-well plate format, with each condition having at least 4 biological replicates.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 12 hours to measure final intracellular glutamate concentrations.

5. **Controls:**
   - Include wells with no glutamate and no zinc dichloride to serve as baseline growth controls.
   - Include wells with only zinc dichloride (without glutamate) to assess the independent effect of zinc dichloride on growth.

### Expected Outcomes
- **Increased Glutamate Levels:**
  - Expect a decrease in growth rate or final cell density in the presence of zinc dichloride compared to control glutamate levels, supporting the hypothesis of decreased metal resistance.
  
- **Control Conditions:**
  - Cells without zinc dichloride should show normal growth irrespective of glutamate levels, confirming that the observed effects are specific to zinc dichloride treatment.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - High zinc dichloride concentrations should reduce growth in all conditions, but the effect should be more pronounced in the high glutamate condition if the hypothesis is correct.

- **Verification:**
  - Mass-spectrometry results should show a clear increase in intracellular glutamate in supplemented conditions, verifying successful modulation.

If the expected outcomes are observed, it would support the statement that higher intracellular glutamate levels are associated with decreased resistance to zinc dichloride. If not, further investigation into other potential mechanisms or confounding factors would be warranted.
