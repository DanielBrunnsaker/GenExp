**Selected Statement:**
Cells with lower than normal levels of intracellular methionine in standard conditions (grown on minimal media without any amino acids) associate with the following phenotype, described as a prolog program: 
```prolog
Cell(A):-exhibits_phenotype(A,'decreased resistance to chemicals',B,C),compound_name(B,quinine).
```

**Motivation:**
This statement is selected because it provides a specific and testable association between intracellular methionine levels and resistance to a specific chemical, quinine. Quinine is a well-known compound that can be used to assess chemical resistance, and its effects on yeast cells can be quantified. The statement is also novel and has potential implications for understanding the role of methionine in chemical resistance mechanisms.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with lower than normal levels of intracellular methionine will exhibit decreased resistance to quinine, as measured by growth inhibition in the presence of quinine compared to control cells with normal methionine levels.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media Preparation:**
   - Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Prepare minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Methionine Modulation:**
   - Supplement the media with varying concentrations of methionine to create three conditions:
     - Low methionine: 0.01 mM
     - Normal methionine (control): 0.1 mM
     - High methionine: 1.0 mM

3. **Quinine Treatment:**
   - Prepare quinine treatment at three concentrations to evaluate resistance:
     - Low quinine: 0.1 mM
     - Medium quinine: 0.5 mM
     - High quinine: 1.0 mM

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - At t=0h, add methionine and quinine according to the defined conditions.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for 12 hours.

5. **Controls:**
   - Include no-quinine control for each methionine level to assess the independent effect of methionine on growth.
   - Include no-methionine control with quinine to assess the baseline resistance.

6. **Verification of Methionine Levels:**
   - At experiment termination, use mass-spectrometry based metabolomics to confirm intracellular methionine concentrations.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- If the hypothesis is correct, cells with low methionine levels will show greater growth inhibition in the presence of quinine compared to cells with normal methionine levels.
- Cells with high methionine levels may exhibit similar or increased resistance to quinine compared to normal levels, indicating a protective effect of methionine.
- The no-quinine controls will help establish the baseline growth effect of methionine supplementation alone.
- Mass-spectrometry results should confirm that intracellular methionine levels correspond to the supplementation conditions.

By testing these outcomes, we can support or refute the statement that lower methionine levels associate with decreased resistance to quinine, providing insights into the role of methionine in chemical resistance.
