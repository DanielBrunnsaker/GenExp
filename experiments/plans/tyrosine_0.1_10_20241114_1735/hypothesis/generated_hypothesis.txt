Selected Statement: 
```prolog
Cell(A):-exhibits_phenotype(A,'decreased resistance to chemicals',B,C),compound_name(B,'sodium dodecyl sulfate').
```

Motivation:
The selected statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of tyrosine and decreased resistance to sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), a commonly used detergent. This is a feasible experiment because SDS is well-characterized and can be easily incorporated into yeast growth assays. The novelty lies in exploring the relationship between amino acid metabolism and chemical resistance, which could reveal insights into stress response mechanisms in yeast. Additionally, this experiment can be safely executed under standard laboratory conditions.

Hypothesis:
Increasing intracellular tyrosine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae will result in decreased resistance to sodium dodecyl sulfate under standard growth conditions.

Experimental Plan:
1. **Strain and Growth Conditions:** Use a wild-type Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain. Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids at 30°C with shaking.

2. **Tyrosine Modulation:** 
   - Control: No tyrosine supplementation.
   - Low Tyrosine: Supplement with 0.1 mM L-tyrosine.
   - High Tyrosine: Supplement with 1.0 mM L-tyrosine.
   
   Verify the effect of tyrosine supplementation on intracellular levels using mass-spectrometry based metabolomics at the end of the experiment.

3. **SDS Treatment:**
   - Control: No SDS.
   - Low SDS: 0.01% SDS.
   - High SDS: 0.05% SDS.

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Prepare 96-well plates with each well containing 200 µL of media.
   - Inoculate each well to an initial OD600 of 0.05.
   - Apply tyrosine and SDS treatments at t=0h.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes for 12 hours.

5. **Controls and Verification:**
   - Include wells with only tyrosine supplementation to assess its independent effect on growth.
   - Include wells with only SDS to determine baseline resistance.
   - Conduct metabolomics to confirm intracellular tyrosine levels.

6. **Data Analysis:**
   - Compare growth curves across different conditions.
   - Determine IC50 values for SDS in each tyrosine condition.

Expected Outcomes:
- If the hypothesis is correct, wells with higher tyrosine levels will show reduced growth in the presence of SDS compared to controls, indicating decreased resistance.
- Wells with only tyrosine supplementation should show normal growth, confirming that tyrosine alone does not inhibit growth.
- Wells with only SDS should demonstrate a concentration-dependent growth inhibition, establishing a baseline resistance profile.
- Metabolomics should confirm increased intracellular tyrosine levels in supplemented conditions.

By systematically varying tyrosine and SDS concentrations, we aim to either support or refute the association between high intracellular tyrosine levels and decreased resistance to SDS, while controlling for independent effects of each variable.
