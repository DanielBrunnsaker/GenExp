### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular serine levels and decreased resistance to the chemical 4-nitroquinoline N-oxide (4-NQO) in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Understanding this association is important for elucidating the role of serine metabolism in stress resistance. By investigating this relationship, we can gain insights into how serine impacts cellular responses to chemical stressors.

### Hypothesis

Cells with higher intracellular serine levels exhibit decreased resistance to 4-nitroquinoline N-oxide due to serine's involvement in metabolic pathways that modulate stress responses. Specifically, increased serine may impact the synthesis of compounds that are crucial for detoxifying or repairing damage caused by 4-NQO, potentially through alterations in the one-carbon metabolism or antioxidant defense systems.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Control Setup:**
   - Inoculate Saccharomyces cerevisiae reference strain in minimal media (without amino acids) at an initial OD600 of 0.05. Incubate at 30°C with shaking.

2. **Serine Supplementation:**
   - Prepare media with three levels of serine supplementation:
     - Control: No serine added.
     - Low Serine: 50 µM serine.
     - High Serine: 200 µM serine.
   - Justification: Serine is known to be involved in one-carbon metabolism and can influence cellular redox states, potentially affecting stress responses.

3. **4-NQO Treatment:**
   - At t=0h, add 4-NQO to all cultures at a concentration of 1 µM, a concentration known to induce stress without completely inhibiting growth.

4. **Growth and Viability Measurement:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability over the 14-hour period.

5. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At the end of the experiment, perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular serine levels and other relevant metabolites.

6. **Assumption Verification:**
   - Include a parallel set of experiments without 4-NQO to assess the independent effect of serine supplementation on growth and metabolite levels.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Intracellular Serine:**
  - If higher serine levels are indeed associated with decreased resistance to 4-NQO, we expect to see reduced growth rates and viability in cultures with higher serine supplementation compared to the control when exposed to 4-NQO.
  - Metabolomics should show elevated intracellular serine levels in supplemented conditions.

- **Independent Effects of Serine:**
  - In the absence of 4-NQO, serine supplementation might independently affect growth due to its role in biosynthetic pathways, potentially showing either enhanced or unchanged growth rates depending on its effect on cellular metabolism.

- **Verification of Assumptions:**
  - The parallel experiment without 4-NQO will help determine if serine alone affects growth, ensuring that any observed effects in the presence of 4-NQO are specifically due to the interaction between serine levels and 4-NQO stress.

By carefully controlling and measuring these variables, the experiment will provide insights into the relationship between serine levels and chemical stress resistance in yeast.
