### Motivation

The statement suggests that higher intracellular serine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae are associated with normal resistance to the antifungal compound ketoconazole. Understanding this association could provide insights into the metabolic pathways involved in antifungal resistance and help identify potential targets for improving resistance through metabolic engineering.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular serine levels exhibit normal resistance to ketoconazole due to serine's role in the synthesis of phospholipids and sphingolipids, which are crucial for maintaining cell membrane integrity. This integrity may contribute to the cell's ability to resist chemical stress imposed by ketoconazole.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cultures in minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.
   - Incubate all cultures at 30°C with shaking.

2. **Supplementation:**
   - **Control Group:** No supplementation.
   - **Low Serine Group:** Supplement with 0.1 mM serine.
   - **High Serine Group:** Supplement with 1 mM serine.
   - Justification: Serine is a precursor for phospholipid synthesis, and varying its concentration can modulate intracellular levels, potentially affecting membrane integrity and resistance.

3. **Ketoconazole Treatment:**
   - After supplementation, add ketoconazole to each well at a concentration of 10 µg/mL at t=0h to test resistance.
   - Justification: This concentration is known to affect yeast growth, allowing for the assessment of resistance.

4. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.
   - At the end of 14 hours, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular serine concentrations and other relevant metabolites.

5. **Controls and Assumptions:**
   - Include wells with ketoconazole but without serine supplementation to assess baseline resistance.
   - Include wells with serine supplementation but without ketoconazole to evaluate the independent effect of serine on growth.
   - Assumption: Serine supplementation will effectively increase intracellular serine levels without causing toxicity at the specified concentrations.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Group:** Moderate growth inhibition by ketoconazole, reflecting baseline resistance.
- **Low Serine Group:** Slightly improved growth compared to control if serine contributes to resistance.
- **High Serine Group:** Significantly improved growth, indicating enhanced resistance due to higher serine levels supporting membrane integrity.
- **Independent Effects:** Serine supplementation without ketoconazole should show normal growth, confirming that serine itself does not inhibit growth.

If the hypothesis is correct, increased serine levels will correlate with improved resistance to ketoconazole, supporting the association described in the statement. Any observed changes in resistance should be linked to altered intracellular serine levels verified through metabolomics.
