### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular serine levels and normal resistance to the antifungal compound ketoconazole in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Serine is a non-essential amino acid involved in various metabolic pathways, including protein synthesis and one-carbon metabolism. Understanding this association could provide insights into metabolic adaptations that confer resistance to chemical stressors.

### Hypothesis

Cells with elevated intracellular serine levels exhibit normal resistance to ketoconazole due to enhanced metabolic flexibility or stress response pathways. Increased serine may support membrane integrity or detoxification processes that mitigate the effects of ketoconazole.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Use a reference prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids at 30°C with shaking.
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05.
   - Terminate the experiment at 14 hours.

2. **Serine Supplementation:**
   - Prepare three conditions to modulate intracellular serine levels:
     - Control: No serine supplementation.
     - Low serine: Supplement with 0.5 mM serine.
     - High serine: Supplement with 2 mM serine.
   - Justification: These concentrations are physiologically relevant and likely to modulate intracellular serine levels without causing toxicity.

3. **Ketoconazole Treatment:**
   - Prepare media with 0.5 µg/mL ketoconazole, a sub-lethal concentration allowing assessment of resistance.
   - Include ketoconazole in all serine supplementation conditions to evaluate its effect on resistance.

4. **Control Experiments:**
   - Grow cultures without ketoconazole for each serine condition to assess the independent effect of serine on growth.

5. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and calculate growth rates.
   - At experiment termination, use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular serine concentrations.

6. **Assumption Verification:**
   - Conduct a parallel set of experiments to confirm that serine supplementation effectively increases intracellular serine levels under these conditions.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Intracellular Serine:**
  - Higher serine conditions should show increased intracellular serine levels in metabolomics data.

- **Growth and Resistance:**
  - If serine enhances resistance, cultures with higher serine levels should exhibit growth rates similar to control cultures in the presence of ketoconazole.
  - Independent of ketoconazole, serine may affect growth due to its role in metabolism, which should be observed in control experiments without ketoconazole.

- **Association Validation:**
  - If the hypothesis is correct, there will be a correlation between increased serine levels and normal resistance to ketoconazole. If no correlation is observed, it may suggest other factors contribute to the resistance phenotype.

By implementing this experimental plan, we aim to explore the association between intracellular serine levels and resistance to ketoconazole, providing insights into potential metabolic or stress response mechanisms.
