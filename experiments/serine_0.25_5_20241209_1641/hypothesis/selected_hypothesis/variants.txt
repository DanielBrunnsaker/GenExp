####### OPTION 1 #######

**Motivation:**
The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular serine levels and normal resistance to ketoconazole in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Ketoconazole is an antifungal agent that disrupts cell membrane integrity by inhibiting ergosterol synthesis. Understanding this association can provide insights into metabolic adaptations that confer chemical resistance.

**Hypothesis:**
Cells with elevated intracellular serine levels exhibit normal resistance to ketoconazole due to serine's role in cellular processes that enhance membrane integrity or stress response pathways. Serine might influence the synthesis of phospholipids or other cellular components that mitigate ketoconazole's effects.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Culture Conditions:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic strain of S. cerevisiae.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids to ensure baseline conditions.

2. **Serine Supplementation:**
   - Prepare three media conditions with varying serine concentrations:
     - Control: 0 mM serine.
     - Low serine: 0.5 mM serine.
     - High serine: 2.0 mM serine.
   - Justification: These concentrations are chosen to cover a range of physiological serine levels without causing toxicity or growth inhibition.

3. **Ketoconazole Treatment:**
   - Add ketoconazole at a concentration of 10 µg/mL at the start of the experiment for all conditions.
   - Justification: This concentration is known to exert selective pressure on yeast cells without completely inhibiting growth.

4. **Experimental Setup:**
   - Inoculate cultures at an initial OD600 of 0.05 in 96-well plates.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking to ensure aeration and homogeneity.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and assess viability.

5. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At experiment termination (14 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular serine levels.
   - This will confirm the modulation of serine levels across the different conditions.

6. **Control Experiments:**
   - Conduct parallel experiments without ketoconazole to assess the independent effect of serine on growth and viability.
   - Include additional wells with ketoconazole but without serine supplementation to establish a baseline resistance level.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Increased Serine Levels:**
  - If higher intracellular serine levels lead to normal resistance, cells in the high serine condition should exhibit similar growth rates to control conditions despite ketoconazole presence.
  - Metabolomics should confirm elevated serine levels in supplemented conditions.

- **Independent Effects of Serine:**
  - Serine supplementation alone may enhance growth or alter metabolic profiles, observable in control experiments without ketoconazole.
  - Any growth improvement in the absence of ketoconazole will need to be considered when interpreting resistance data.

- **Control Conditions:**
  - Cells in minimal media without serine supplementation are expected to show reduced growth in the presence of ketoconazole, serving as a baseline for resistance comparison.

By carefully modulating serine levels and assessing their impact on ketoconazole resistance, this experimental design aims to elucidate the potential protective role of serine in yeast cells.
