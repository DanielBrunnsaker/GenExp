**Motivation:**
The aim of this experiment is to investigate the association between intracellular valine levels and increased oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae when exposed to paraquat, a known inducer of oxidative stress. Understanding this relationship could provide insights into the metabolic pathways that contribute to stress resistance, potentially revealing targets for enhancing stress tolerance in yeast.

**Hypothesis:**
Higher intracellular levels of valine enhance the oxidative stress resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae when exposed to paraquat, possibly by modulating antioxidant pathways or stress response mechanisms.

**Experimental Plan:**

1. **Strain and Media:**
   - Use a reference/wild-type prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Baseline media: Minimal media without amino acids.

2. **Supplementation Levels:**
   - Valine supplementation at two concentrations:
     - Control: 0 mM (no supplementation).
     - Physiological level: 2 mM.
     - Supraphysiological level: 10 mM.

3. **Oxidative Stress Treatment:**
   - Paraquat treatment at a concentration of 1 mM to induce oxidative stress.
   - Control: No paraquat treatment.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use lysine as a negative control at an equimolar concentration to the highest valine supplementation (10 mM).

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total conditions: 3 valine levels x 2 paraquat levels + 2 negative controls = 8 conditions.
   - Incubate all conditions at 30°C with shaking.
   - Add all supplementations and treatments at the start of the experiment (t=0h).

6. **Measurements:**
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth and viability.
   - At experiment termination (20 hours), perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular valine concentrations.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- **Increased Valine Levels:**
  - It is expected that higher concentrations of valine will correlate with increased oxidative stress resistance in the presence of paraquat, as indicated by better growth and viability compared to controls.
  - The control condition (0 mM valine with paraquat) should show reduced growth due to oxidative stress.

- **Negative Control:**
  - The lysine control should not significantly alter oxidative stress resistance, confirming that the observed effects are specific to valine.

- **Independent Effects:**
  - The presence of paraquat is expected to decrease growth in all conditions, but the extent of this decrease should be less in conditions with higher valine supplementation if the hypothesis is correct.

This experimental setup will allow us to assess the association between valine levels and oxidative stress resistance, while controlling for potential confounders and ensuring the specificity of the observed effects.
