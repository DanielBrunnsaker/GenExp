### Motivation
The statement suggests that higher intracellular levels of valine are associated with increased oxidative stress resistance, as tested using paraquat, a known inducer of oxidative stress. The goal of this experiment is to determine whether increasing intracellular valine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae enhances resistance to oxidative stress induced by paraquat.

### Hypothesis
Increased intracellular levels of valine enhance oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae by modulating cellular pathways that mitigate oxidative damage. This can be tested by supplementing media with valine and assessing the yeast's growth and viability in the presence of paraquat.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Base Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the base for all conditions.

2. **Valine Supplementation Levels:**
   - Control (0 mM valine)
   - Physiologically relevant concentration (1 mM valine)
   - Supraphysiological concentration (10 mM valine)

3. **Paraquat Treatment Levels:**
   - Control (0 mM paraquat)
   - Treatment (0.5 mM paraquat)

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use lysine as a negative control (10 mM lysine) in conditions without valine and paraquat.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - For each valine supplementation level, test with and without paraquat, resulting in the following conditions:
     1. 0 mM valine + 0 mM paraquat
     2. 0 mM valine + 0.5 mM paraquat
     3. 1 mM valine + 0 mM paraquat
     4. 1 mM valine + 0.5 mM paraquat
     5. 10 mM valine + 0 mM paraquat
     6. 10 mM valine + 0.5 mM paraquat
   - Include negative control with lysine:
     7. 10 mM lysine + 0 mM paraquat
     8. 10 mM lysine + 0.5 mM paraquat

6. **Experimental Execution:**
   - Inoculate all wells with an equal concentration of S. cerevisiae cells.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Terminate the experiment at 20 hours.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular valine levels at the end of the experiment.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Valine Levels:** If higher intracellular valine levels confer increased oxidative stress resistance, we expect to see better growth and viability in conditions with 0.5 mM paraquat and higher valine concentrations (1 mM and 10 mM) compared to the control (0 mM valine with paraquat).
- **Negative Control:** The lysine control should show no improvement in oxidative stress resistance when exposed to paraquat, confirming that the observed effect is specific to valine.
- **Independent Effects:** Growth patterns in conditions without paraquat will help determine any independent effects of valine supplementation on yeast growth, ensuring that any observed resistance is not due to general growth enhancement.
