### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of valine and increased oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae when exposed to paraquat. Paraquat is a known inducer of oxidative stress, and understanding how valine levels influence stress resistance could provide insights into metabolic regulation and stress response mechanisms.

### Hypothesis

Increased intracellular valine levels enhance the oxidative stress resistance of Saccharomyces cerevisiae when exposed to paraquat. This may occur due to valine's role in modulating cellular metabolism, potentially influencing pathways involved in oxidative stress defense.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Strain and Media**: 
   - Use a wild-type, prototrophic Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain.
   - Grow cells in minimal media without amino acids unless specified otherwise.

2. **Valine Supplementation Levels**:
   - Control: No valine supplementation.
   - Low: 50 µM valine.
   - High: 500 µM valine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Paraquat Treatment Levels**:
   - Control: No paraquat.
   - Treatment: 1 mM paraquat to induce oxidative stress.

4. **Negative Control**:
   - Use lysine at 500 µM as a negative control, as it is not directly involved in oxidative stress pathways.

5. **Experimental Setup**:
   - Total conditions: 3 valine levels x 2 paraquat levels + 2 lysine controls = 8 conditions.
   - Each condition will be tested in a separate set of wells in a 96-well plate format.
   - All supplementations and treatments are added at t=0h.

6. **Measurements**:
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.
   - Use mass spectrometry-based metabolomics at 20 hours to measure intracellular valine concentrations.
   - Assess viability and oxidative stress resistance by comparing growth curves under paraquat treatment.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Increased Valine Levels**: 
  - If increased valine enhances oxidative stress resistance, expect higher growth rates and viability in the high valine + paraquat condition compared to the control valine + paraquat condition.
  - Intracellular valine concentrations should correlate with increased resistance.

- **Paraquat Treatment**:
  - Reduced growth and viability in paraquat-treated conditions compared to untreated controls, highlighting oxidative stress effects.

- **Negative Control (Lysine)**:
  - Lysine supplementation should not significantly alter oxidative stress resistance, serving as a baseline for comparison.

By comparing the growth and viability across different conditions, the experiment will elucidate the potential role of valine in modulating oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
