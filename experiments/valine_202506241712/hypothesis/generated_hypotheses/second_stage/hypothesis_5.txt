### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular valine levels and increased oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, particularly when exposed to paraquat. Valine is a branched-chain amino acid that could potentially influence stress response pathways. Investigating this association can provide insights into the metabolic and stress response mechanisms within yeast cells.

### Hypothesis

Increased intracellular valine levels enhance oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae by modulating antioxidant pathways, which could lead to increased survival and growth rates under paraquat-induced oxidative stress conditions.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Baseline Conditions:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the baseline condition.

2. **Valine Supplementation:**
   - Level 1 (Control): No valine supplementation.
   - Level 2: 5 mM valine.
   - Level 3: 20 mM valine (supraphysiological concentration).

3. **Oxidative Stress Induction:**
   - Paraquat Treatment:
     - Level 1 (Control): No paraquat.
     - Level 2: 0.1 mM paraquat.

4. **Negative Control:**
   - Use lysine at 20 mM as a negative control, as lysine is not directly involved in the pathways associated with oxidative stress resistance.

5. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Total combinations: 3 valine levels x 2 paraquat levels + negative control = 7 conditions.
   - Each condition will be set up in a 96-well plate format with appropriate replicates.

6. **Measurements:**
   - Monitor OD600 every 20 minutes to assess growth.
   - Terminate experiment at 20 hours.
   - Perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular valine and other relevant metabolites.
   - Assess cell viability and oxidative stress markers post-experiment.

### Expected Outcomes

- **Control Conditions (No Valine, No Paraquat):** Baseline growth and stress resistance.
- **Increased Valine Levels (No Paraquat):** Potential increase in growth due to enhanced metabolic activity.
- **Paraquat Treatment (No Valine):** Reduced growth and viability due to oxidative stress.
- **Increased Valine + Paraquat:** If the hypothesis holds, expect improved growth and viability compared to paraquat-only treatment, indicating increased oxidative stress resistance.
- **Negative Control (Lysine + Paraquat):** No significant change in oxidative stress resistance compared to paraquat-only treatment, confirming that lysine does not influence the stress response pathway.

This experimental setup will test whether valine supplementation can enhance oxidative stress resistance in yeast and provide insights into the metabolic pathways involved.
