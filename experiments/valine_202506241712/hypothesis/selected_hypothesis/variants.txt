####### OPTION 1 #######

### Motivation

The statement suggests an association between higher intracellular levels of valine and increased oxidative stress resistance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Paraquat is a known inducer of oxidative stress, which allows us to use it to test this association. By modulating intracellular valine levels and subjecting the cells to paraquat-induced oxidative stress, we can evaluate whether higher valine levels correlate with increased resistance.

### Hypothesis

Increasing intracellular valine levels in Saccharomyces cerevisiae enhances resistance to paraquat-induced oxidative stress by potentially modulating antioxidant pathways or stress response mechanisms.

### Experimental Plan

1. **Media Preparation:**
   - Use minimal media without amino acids as the base.
   - Prepare three valine supplementation levels:
     - Control: 0 mM valine
     - Physiological level: 0.5 mM valine
     - Supraphysiological level: 5 mM valine

2. **Negative Control:**
   - Include lysine as a negative control at 5 mM, equimolar to the highest valine concentration, to ensure any effects are specific to valine.

3. **Paraquat Treatment:**
   - Prepare two paraquat treatment levels:
     - Control: 0 mM paraquat
     - Treatment: 1 mM paraquat (a known concentration to induce oxidative stress in yeast)

4. **Experimental Conditions:**
   - Combine valine supplementation levels with paraquat treatment levels:
     - 0 mM valine + 0 mM paraquat
     - 0 mM valine + 1 mM paraquat
     - 0.5 mM valine + 0 mM paraquat
     - 0.5 mM valine + 1 mM paraquat
     - 5 mM valine + 0 mM paraquat
     - 5 mM valine + 1 mM paraquat
   - Include lysine negative control conditions:
     - 5 mM lysine + 0 mM paraquat
     - 5 mM lysine + 1 mM paraquat

5. **Culture Conditions:**
   - Inoculate each well with a standardized concentration of the wild-type prototrophic strain.
   - Incubate at 30°C with shaking.
   - Measure OD600 every 20 minutes to monitor growth.

6. **Metabolomics Analysis:**
   - At 20 hours, terminate the experiment and perform mass spectrometry-based metabolomics to measure intracellular valine concentrations.

### Expected Outcomes

- If higher valine levels confer increased oxidative stress resistance, we expect to see:
  - Improved growth rates and viability in the 0.5 mM and 5 mM valine conditions with paraquat compared to the 0 mM valine condition with paraquat.
  - No significant change in growth in the lysine control with paraquat, supporting valine's specific role.
- If valine does not affect oxidative stress resistance:
  - Similar growth inhibition in all paraquat-treated conditions regardless of valine supplementation.
- Independent effects of valine supplementation may include altered growth rates or metabolite profiles in non-stressed conditions, which should be accounted for during data analysis.
